## ACT 1 · THE DESIGN PARADOX

**Beat 1.1: Cold open into the paradox**
```VO
[serious tone] To answer that... we first have to understand a paradox.

Walk into the offices of almost any major chip company on Earth — Intel... Qualcomm... N-Vidia... A-M-D — and you'll find something surprising. [pause] A huge share of the engineers designing their chips... aren't in California.

They're in Bengaluru. In Hyderabad. In Noida.
```

**Beat 1.2: The talent fact** ⚑
```VO
India is home to roughly one in five of the world's semiconductor design engineers. [pause] Some of the most advanced processors on the planet were partly architected... verified... and tested... on Indian desks.

And yet — for decades — almost none of that silicon was actually made in India.
```

**Beat 1.3: The round trip**
```VO
[matter-of-fact] Here's how it works.

An engineer in Bengaluru designs a chip. The finished blueprint — a file — is sent across the ocean, to a foundry in Taiwan... or South Korea. The chip is printed there, on a wafer of pure silicon.

Then it's shipped again — to Malaysia, or China — to be cut, packaged, and tested.

And finally... it comes back to India. Inside a phone. A car. A laptop. A satellite.

[pause] India designs the brain... and then buys it back.
```

**Beat 1.4: The cost of the paradox** ⚑
```VO
That round trip is expensive. Electronics has become one of India's biggest import bills — sitting right alongside crude oil and gold.

And in twenty-twenty-one, the country learned what that dependence really costs... when the chips simply stopped coming.
```

**Beat 1.5: Design is not manufacturing** ⚑
```VO
So why doesn't India just... make them?

Because designing a chip and manufacturing a chip are two completely different businesses.

Design needs brilliant people... and powerful computers. It scales like software.

Manufacturing is something else entirely. [pause] It's heavy industry — performed at the scale of atoms. A single leading-edge factory can cost more than twenty billion dollars... and take years to build before it ships a single chip.

[serious tone] And India... has tried this before.

To understand why it failed... we need to step inside a chip fab.
```

## ACT 2 · WHY CHIP FABS ARE SO BRUTALLY DIFFICULT

**Beat 2.1: Thesis**
```VO
[serious tone] A modern semiconductor fab is arguably the most demanding factory humans have ever built.

It doesn't just need money. It needs four things that most places on Earth simply cannot guarantee — perfect air... perfect water... perfect power... and perfect patience.
```

**Beat 2.2: Air (cleanrooms)** ⚑
```VO
Start with the air.

The transistors on a modern chip are measured in nanometers. A single human hair is roughly eighty thousand nanometers wide. [pause] At that scale... one speck of dust isn't dirt. It's a boulder.

So chips are made inside cleanrooms — spaces thousands of times cleaner than a hospital operating theatre. The air is filtered and replaced every few seconds. Workers move in sealed "bunny suits"... and robots carry the wafers between machines — because even a human breath is a contamination risk.
```

**Beat 2.3: Water** ⚑
```VO
Next... water.

Every wafer is rinsed again and again between steps — with ultrapure water. Water so stripped of minerals and particles... that it is far purer than anything that comes out of a tap.

And a large fab doesn't need a little of it. [pause] It can consume tens of millions of litres a day — as much as a mid-sized city.

[matter-of-fact] Now picture that... in a country where many cities still face summer water shortages.
```

**Beat 2.4: Power** ⚑
```VO
Then... power.

In December, twenty-ten, a power dip lasting just seventy milliseconds hit a Toshiba memory plant in Japan. [pause] Seventy milliseconds. Less than the blink of an eye.

It was enough to disrupt production for months — and to cut a large share of the plant's shipments.

[serious tone] A fab can't just have electricity. It needs power that never flickers. Not for a second. Not for years.
```

**Beat 2.5: Patience — machines, steps, people** ⚑
```VO
And finally... patience.

A single chip can pass through hundreds — sometimes more than a thousand — separate process steps. One wafer can spend months inside the fab.

The most advanced lithography machines come from essentially one company on Earth — A-S-M-L in the Netherlands — and they cost hundreds of millions of dollars each.

And none of it runs without thousands of process engineers — people who've spent years learning how to keep the yield high... in a factory where one bad batch can wipe out millions.

[pause] Brilliant chip designers... aren't automatically brilliant chip makers.
```

**Beat 2.6: The graveyard of attempts** ⚑
```VO
India learned that the hard way.

[matter-of-fact] In the nineteen-eighties, the government built the Semi-Conductor Laboratory in Mohali, Punjab. It wasn't that far behind the global leaders of the day.

Then, in February, nineteen-eighty-nine... a fire tore through the facility. [pause] By the time it was rebuilt... the world had moved on.

In the twenty-tens, two large fab projects were approved — one led by Jaypee Group, with I-B-M and Tower Jazz... another led by Hindustan Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation, with S-T Microelectronics. Neither one was ever built.

And in twenty-twenty-two, Vedanta and Foxconn announced a nearly twenty-billion-dollar chip venture in Gujarat. [pause] Within a year... Foxconn walked away.
```

**Beat 2.7: The lesson**
```VO
[serious tone] Decades of attempts. Billions announced. And still... no commercial chip fab.

The lesson was brutal, but clear: money alone doesn't build a semiconductor industry.

So this time... India changed the playbook.
```

## ACT 3 · THE NEW PLAYBOOK

**Beat 3.1: The mission** ⚑
```VO
In December, twenty-twenty-one — in the middle of the chip shortage — the Union Cabinet approved the India Semiconductor Mission... with an outlay of seventy-six thousand crore rupees. Roughly ten billion dollars.

The offer to chipmakers was simple. [pause] Build here... and the central government will cover up to half of your project cost. Many states add even more on top.

But the money wasn't the real change. [pause] The strategy was.
```

**Beat 3.2: The pivot — don't chase three nanometers**
```VO
India made a quiet... but crucial decision: don't chase the bleeding edge. Not yet.

The headlines are all about three-nanometer chips — the ones inside the latest iPhones and A-I data centres. But here's the thing most people miss.

[matter-of-fact] The vast majority of chips the world uses every day aren't bleeding-edge at all. The chips inside your car... your washing machine... your power grid... your defence systems — most of them are made on older, so-called "mature" nodes. Twenty-eight nanometers... and above.

[pause] Remember those cars stuck in parking lots in twenty-twenty-one? They weren't waiting for cutting-edge processors. They were mostly waiting for cheap, mature-node chips — exactly this kind.
```

**Beat 3.3: Step one — packaging (Micron)** ⚑
```VO
So India started at the most achievable step first: packaging.

In June, twenty-twenty-three, the American memory giant Micron announced an assembly and test plant in Sanand, Gujarat — a project worth about two-point-seven-five billion dollars, with the central and state governments covering most of the cost.

It doesn't print silicon. It takes finished wafers... cuts them, packages them, and tests them into memory chips.

[pause] It's less glamorous than a fab. But it's faster to build... it trains thousands of workers... and it pulls suppliers into the country.

And in early twenty-twenty-six... Micron's Sanand plant began production.
```

**Beat 3.4: Step two — the first real fab (Tata–PSMC, Dholera)** ⚑
```VO
Then came the big one.

In February, twenty-twenty-four, the government approved India's first commercial chip fab — Tata Electronics, in partnership with Taiwan's Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation — in Dholera, Gujarat.

[matter-of-fact] Around ninety-one thousand crore rupees. Up to fifty thousand wafers a month. Built on twenty-eight to one-hundred-ten nanometer technology — the workhorse nodes for cars, power electronics, displays, and industrial chips.

And notice who India brought in. [pause] Not a rival to T-S-M-C... but a Taiwanese partner — that knows how to run a mature-node fab.
```

**Beat 3.5: The cluster** ⚑
```VO
Around those two anchors... a cluster started forming.

Tata is building a large packaging plant in Jagiroad, Assam. C-G Power, with Japan's Renesas, is building one in Sanand. Kaynes Semicon is building another nearby.

[pause] By mid-twenty-twenty-six, the government counted around a dozen approved chip manufacturing units — and had announced a second phase of the mission... with an even bigger budget.
```

**Beat 3.6: Why now — China plus one** ⚑
```VO
And the timing isn't an accident.

After the pandemic... after the chip shortage... and after Washington began restricting advanced chip technology to China in twenty-twenty-two — global companies started asking one question: [pause] where else can we build?

The industry calls it "China plus one."

[matter-of-fact] India's pitch is hard to ignore. A huge domestic market. A fast-growing electronics assembly base — the same factories that now make iPhones in India. And that deep bench of chip designers... finally sitting next to chip makers.

[serious tone] For the first time... the design paradox has a way out.
```

## CONCLUSION & OUTRO

**Beat 4.1: Realistic outlook** ⚑
```VO
[serious tone] So... will India become the next Taiwan?

Let's be honest. Not this decade.

Taiwan's journey started in the nineteen-seventies. T-S-M-C was founded in nineteen-eighty-seven. It took decades of patient, compounding work to build what exists there today.

India is at the very beginning of that road. The Dholera fab still has to prove it can deliver good yields... reliable water... and power that never flickers — the exact problems that sank every attempt before it.
```

**Beat 4.2: What success actually looks like**
```VO
But success doesn't mean beating T-S-M-C.

It means that the next time a crisis hits... the cars on India's parking lots aren't waiting on a ship from across the ocean. [pause] It means mature chips — made at home. A packaging hub the world trusts. And a generation of engineers who don't just design the brain... but build it.

[pause] That... is the real ten-billion-dollar gamble.
```
