[DAE | LOK SABHA 2026]
There's a magnet in your pocket right now.
When your phone buzzes,
that's a tiny motor spinning... and inside it sits a tiny,
very strong magnet.
Now check the spec sheet of an electric scooter or car.
Look at the motor line,
and you'll often see two words... permanent magnet.
Same family of magnet, just bigger.
No magnet, no motor.
No motor... no E.V.
And in 2025,
that one magnet hit India's E.V. makers hard.
July, Pune.
Bajaj's electric Chetak factory was making barely half the scooters it made a year earlier.
The reason?
Not a strike.
Not batteries.
That small magnet... and most of them come from China.
In July 2024,
about 20,000 Chetaks a month.
In July 2025... less than 11,000.
And the crazy part?
The raw material for that magnet is lying in the sand on our own beaches
— in Andhra Pradesh, Kerala,
Tamil Nadu and Odisha.
By our own government's count,
India's sands hold about 7 million tonnes of rare earths.
So why can't India make the magnet?
Stay with me, and you'll see three things.
The hard step nobody else wanted to do.
Then, about five minutes in,
the secret inside our sand that kept it locked away.
And at the end, the date you should watch.
First, what is this magnet?
It's called a rare earth magnet.
It's mostly iron.
Add a little boron... and one special metal,
called neodymium.
It's small, but it's very strong.
And in a motor, strength is everything.
Here's why.
A stronger magnet gives you the same push from a smaller motor.
Smaller means lighter.
And lighter means you get more kilometres on one charge.
Makes sense?
That's why the spec sheet says permanent magnet.
Most electric scooters and cars on the road use these magnets in their motors.
And it's not just vehicles.
Remember the buzz in your phone?
Same kind of magnet.
You'll also find it in many wind turbines,
and in defence systems.
Now look at this number.
India needs about 4,000 tonnes of these magnets a year... and we buy almost all of them from abroad.
