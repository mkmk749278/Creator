Four months later, the new American government scrapped that rule. But India had seen it. The tap is in someone else's hand.

So India's whole mission... ten thousand crore, over five years. Meta alone... thirteen lakh crore, in one single year. That's not double. That's more than a hundred times as much.

So is it hopeless? No. Because remember DeepSeek.

DeepSeek says it trained its base model on about two thousand chips. Not hundreds of thousands. And they were a cut-down version, made so they could still be sold to China.

So what's the lesson for you and me? You don't always need the biggest room. You need clever engineering. And that's a game Indian engineers can play.

April twenty twenty-five. The government picks the first company to build India's own model from scratch. A Bengaluru startup called Sarvam.

Sarvam was started by two Indians you should know, Vivek Raghavan and Pratyush Kumar. The mission gives them about four thousand of those Nvidia chips to work with.

But a month later, the doubts begin. Sarvam's next model is built on top of a French one. And critics ask — if the base is foreign, how is it Indian?

Fair question, isn't it? You'd ask it too. Can an Indian team train one from zero, start to finish, at home?

February twenty twenty-six. Bharat Mandapam, Delhi. The India A.I. Impact Summit.

Then Sarvam did something generous. It made the model open. Any Indian developer can download it and build their own apps on it, for free.

And that same week, BharatGen — a team led by I.I.T. Bombay — showed its own model too, called Param Two, built for all twenty-two scheduled Indian languages.

Now remember my promise to you. The chopping test. Same question. Will it rain in my village tomorrow?

DeepSeek's chopper, you've seen. Hindi, eighteen. Marathi, twenty. Telugu, twenty-five.

The chopper behind Chat G.P.T.'s recent models... Hindi eight, Marathi fourteen, Telugu fifteen.

And Sarvam's? Hindi, eight — exactly the same as English. Marathi, ten. Telugu, eleven.

And this isn't one lucky sentence. We ran the same test on more than a thousand sentences, each one in thirteen languages.

So for your language, the gap shrinks, a lot. Because this time... an Indian team put the missing pieces in.

And it's already at work. Sarvam says its models help run Aadhaar services in Indian languages. And in October, Maharashtra gave Sarvam's A.I. assistant to two thousand five hundred government officials.

So has India built its own Chat G.P.T.? Let me be honest with you. Not one that matches the best in the world. Not yet.

DeepSeek's newest big model has one point six trillion of those dials in total. About fifteen times Sarvam's.

And Sarvam says it plainly, in its own report. The newest models from other labs are trained on far bigger clusters, with far bigger budgets.

So here's the lesson. A machine speaks best in the language of the people who build it.

If India only rents other countries' machines, you and I will keep paying extra to be understood... in our own languages.

And A.I. isn't the only thing India has built for itself. Did you know India has its own satellite navigation, up in space? It's called Navik. So why does your phone still lean on America's G.P.S.? That's our next story.
