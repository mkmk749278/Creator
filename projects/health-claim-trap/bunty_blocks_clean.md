And you wonder, why did I pay insurance all these years if they abandon me the moment I need them? What your insurance agent never told you... is that under official regulations in India, there is a legal rule that makes this exact denial illegal, once your policy has crossed five years. ## Block 2 · 1,948 characters · lines E013–E027

First... who actually says no to you at the hospital desk? Most people think it's the insurance company sitting there evaluating your medical reports. But in reality, it's a middleman called a T-P-A... Third Party Administrator. Many think a T-P-A is a neutral government doctor. It is not. A T-P-A is a company hired and paid by the insurance company to process medical bills. In India, about seven out of every ten health claims are settled through T-P-As. Under I-R-D-A-I rules, no claim can be rejected without the approval of the insurance company's own claims review committee. But here are the three excuses you will often see on these letters. Excuse Number One. Non-disclosure of pre-existing condition. Suppose you were admitted for a broken leg or dengue. A junior doctor writes one line in your notes. Patient has mild blood pressure. The reviewer sees that one line. He opens your proposal form from five years ago, and says... You didn't declare blood pressure. Cashless denied. Wait... does blood pressure cause a broken leg or a dengue infection? Of course not. But in the first five years of your policy, the company can still use it. After five years, the rule changes. Excuse Number Two. Not medically necessary. They say your treatment could have been done as an outpatient visit without staying in hospital. And Excuse Number Three. Room rent limit, and proportionate deduction. Say your policy allows a room of five thousand rupees a day, but you took an eight thousand rupee room. They don't just deduct the three thousand rupee difference. They pay only five out of every eight rupees... on the surgery, doctor, and nursing charges too. That is a cut of about thirty-seven percent on those charges. When a family sees that technical rejection letter, they feel completely helpless. They think the door is closed forever. It is not. A cashless denial... is NOT a claim rejection. Your legal right to the money is still alive.

## Block 3 · 1,608 characters · lines E028–E041

Now listen carefully. This may be the single most important rule in Indian health insurance. It is called the moratorium period. Earlier, this period was eight years. But in twenty twenty-four, the I-R-D-A-I brought it down from eight years to five years. That means sixty continuous months in a row, with no break in your policy. So, what does it mean for you? After five years of continuous cover, the insurance company legally CANNOT question your policy or your claim on grounds of non-disclosure, like an old disease you did not mention. Read that again. After five years, an old health history cannot be used as an excuse. There is only one single exception. Proven fraud that the company has to establish. Not a forgotten fever, not an undiagnosed ache... active, deliberate fraud. Your normal policy limits and exclusions still apply, and if you port your policy to another insurer, your years move with you. If you cancel and buy a new one, the count resets to zero. Now think about our family at the billing counter. Six years of continuous policy. A letter that says non-disclosure. That excuse directly violates the I-R-D-A-I rule. And there is one more rule... a clock. When the hospital sends the final discharge request to the insurance company, the company must give final approval within three hours. I-R-D-A-I mandates that the patient should not be made to wait. If the approval comes later than three hours, any extra amount the hospital charges for that delay is paid by the insurance company from its own funds... not by you. Remember this rule, and ask for it at the discharge counter.

## Block 4 · 2,941 characters · lines E042–E064

So, what should you do if your cashless is denied at the counter tonight? Do not shout at hospital staff. Do not panic. Follow these four steps. Step One. Collect your papers before you leave. If you have to pay the bill yourself, pay it. But before leaving the hospital, ask for copies of the discharge summary, the final itemized bill, all test reports, pharmacy receipts, and the written cashless denial letter. If they claimed admission was not needed, ask your treating doctor for a short clinical justification certificate. Step Two. File for reimbursement within fifteen to thirty days of discharge. Check your policy for your exact limit. If they reject it, write a calm, formal email to the company's Grievance Redressal Officer. State your policy number, claim number and amount, and if your policy has crossed five years, quote the I-R-D-A-I five-year moratorium rule. The company must reply within fourteen days. Step Three. Register on Bima Bharosa. If the company ignores you or sends the same generic rejection, go to Bima Bharosa dot I-R-D-A-I dot gov dot in. This is the regulator's own portal, and complaints filed here are tracked directly by the I-R-D-A-I. And Step Four. The Insurance Ombudsman. If the company still refuses after thirty days, you do NOT need a consumer court case, and you do NOT need an expensive lawyer. Go to the official website, c-i-o-i-n-s dot co dot in. The Insurance Ombudsman is a government body set up specifically to resolve insurance disputes. You can file online. It costs zero rupees. You do not need a lawyer. In the financial year twenty twenty-four to twenty-five, the Ombudsman offices received over thirty-two thousand health complaints. Of the cases they settled or decided, over seventy-five percent went in favor of the policyholder. And eight out of ten complaints were closed within three months. The Ombudsman can award up to fifty lakh rupees, and the award is legally binding on the insurance company. Finally... how do you prevent this before you even buy? Rule One. Disclose everything in writing on the proposal form. If you smoke or take mild B-P medication, declare it. Once it's on paper, they can never call it non-disclosure. Rule Two. Choose a policy with No Room Rent Capping. In our example, one bigger room cost almost sixty thousand rupees through proportionate cuts. Rule Three. Ask about a Consumables Cover. Reports say gloves, masks, and syringes can be five to fifteen percent of a hospital bill. Some insurers sell an add-on cover for this. Check its price and its limit before you buy. Remember, you pay for insurance so your family has peace of mind. Know the five-year rule. Use the Ombudsman. And protect your hard-earned savings. Be smart. Be informed. And always... be practical.
