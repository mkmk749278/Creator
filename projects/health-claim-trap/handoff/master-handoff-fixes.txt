CHAPTER 1: The Midnight Bill (bp-hlth01-ch1.mp3)
Imagine this situation... it happens to thousands of middle-class families in India every single
day.

You pay thirty-five thousand rupees every year for health insurance. You renew it on time for five
or six straight years. You never miss a single payment.

Then, an emergency hits. Your loved one is admitted to the hospital.

Three days later, you are standing at the hospital billing counter at nine o'clock at night. The
discharge papers are ready. Your bill is four point five lakh rupees.

And the desk clerk looks up at you and hands you a printed letter saying: "Sorry sir, your
cashless claim was rejected by the T-P-A due to non-disclosure of a pre-existing disease."

[sigh] Your heart drops. Your family panics.

It's night time, the banks are closed, and you are forced to break your fixed deposits, borrow
from relatives, or swipe an emergency credit card just to get your loved one out of the hospital.

And you wonder: "Why did I pay insurance all these years if they abandon me the moment I
need them?"

[pause] What your insurance agent never told you... is that under official regulations in India,
there is a legal rule that makes most of these rejections completely illegal.

In this video, we are not going to shout, and we are not going to promote any insurance policies.

CHAPTER 2: Who Really Says No? (bp-hlth01-ch2.mp3)
First... who actually says no to you at the hospital desk?

Most people think it’s the insurance company sitting there evaluating your medical reports. But
in reality, it’s a middleman called a T-P-A... Third Party Administrator.

Many think a T-P-A is a neutral government doctor. It is not! A T-P-A is a private corporate
agency hired by the insurance company to process medical bills. And let's be honest about how
corporate incentives work.

T-P-A staff handle thousands of files every single day. Their default reaction to any expensive
bill... is to issue an automated query or a quick cashless denial letter.

Under I-R-D-A-I rules, no claim can be rejected without the approval of the insurance company's
own claims review committee. But here are the three excuses you will often see on these letters:

Excuse Number One: "Non-disclosure of pre-existing condition."

Suppose you were admitted for a broken leg or dengue. A junior doctor writes one line in your
notes: "Patient has mild blood pressure."

The reviewer sees that one line. He opens your proposal form from five years ago, and says...
"You didn't declare blood pressure. Cashless denied!"

Wait... does blood pressure cause a broken leg or a dengue infection? Of course not! But they
use it anyway to deny your payout.

Excuse Number Two: "Not medically necessary." They say your treatment could have been
done as an outpatient visit without staying in hospital.

And Excuse Number Three: "Room rent limit, and proportionate deduction."
Say your policy allows a room of five thousand rupees a day, but you took an eight thousand
rupee room. They don't just deduct the three thousand rupee difference!

They pay only five out of every eight rupees... on the surgery, doctor, and nursing charges too!
That is a cut of about thirty-seven percent across your entire bill!

[sigh] When a family sees that technical rejection letter, they feel completely helpless. They
think the door is closed forever.

It is not. A cashless denial... is NOT a claim rejection. Your legal right to the money is still alive.


CHAPTER 3: The 5-Year Rule & The Clock (bp-hlth01-ch3.mp3)
Now listen carefully. This may be the single most important rule in Indian health insurance.

It is called the moratorium period.

Earlier, this period was eight years. But in twenty twenty-four, the I-R-D-A-I brought it down from
eight years to five years.

That means sixty continuous months in a row, with no break in your policy.

So, what does it mean for you?

[pause] After five years of continuous cover, the insurance company legally CANNOT question
your policy or your claim on grounds of non-disclosure or pre-existing diseases!

Read that again: After five years, an old health history cannot be used as an excuse.

There is only one single exception: proven criminal fraud that the company has to establish in
court. Not a forgotten fever, not an undiagnosed ache... active, deliberate fraud.

Your normal policy limits still apply, and if you port your policy to another insurer, your years
move with you. If you cancel and buy a new one, the count resets to zero.

Now think about our family at the billing counter. Six years of continuous policy. A letter that
says non-disclosure. That excuse directly violates the I-R-D-A-I rule!

And there is one more rule... a clock.

When the hospital officially uploads the complete final discharge bill to the insurance portal, the
company must give final approval within exactly three hours.
I-R-D-A-I mandates that the patient should not be made to wait. If the approval comes later than
three hours, any extra room rent the hospital charges for that delay is paid by the insurance
company from its own funds... not by you!

Remember this rule, and ask for it at the discharge counter.


CHAPTER 4: The 4-Step Plan & Buyer Rules (bp-hlth01-ch4.mp3)
So, what should you do if your cashless is denied at the counter tonight?

Do not shout at hospital staff. Do not panic. Follow these four steps.

Step One: Collect your papers before you leave.

If you have to pay the bill yourself, pay it. But before leaving the hospital, ask for copies of: the
discharge summary, the final itemized bill, all test reports, pharmacy receipts, and the written
cashless denial letter. If they claimed admission was not needed, ask your treating doctor for a
short clinical justification certificate.

Step Two: File for reimbursement within fifteen to thirty days.

If they reject it, write a calm, formal email to the company's Principal Nodal Officer—the P-N-O.
State your policy number, claim number, amount, and quote the I-R-D-A-I Five-Year Moratorium
rule. The company must reply within fourteen days.

Step Three: Register on Bima Bharosa.

If the company ignores you or sends the same generic rejection, go to Bima Bharosa dot
I-R-D-A-I dot gov dot in. This is the regulator's own portal, and complaints filed here are tracked
directly by government authorities.

And Step Four: The Insurance Ombudsman.

If the company still refuses after thirty days, do NOT go to a consumer court, and do NOT hire
an expensive lawyer!

Go to the official website: c-i-o-i-n-s dot co dot in.

The Insurance Ombudsman is a government body set up specifically to resolve insurance
disputes. You can file online. It costs zero rupees. You do not need a lawyer.
In the financial year twenty twenty-four to twenty-five, the Ombudsman resolved over thirty-two
thousand health complaints. Over seventy-five percent of decided cases went in favor of the
policyholder! And eight out of ten complaints were closed within three months.

The Ombudsman can award up to fifty lakh rupees, and the award is legally binding on the
insurance company.

Finally... how do you prevent this before you even buy?

Rule One: Disclose everything in writing on the proposal form. If you smoke or take mild B-P
medication, declare it. Once it's on paper, they can never call it non-disclosure.

Rule Two: Choose a policy with "No Room Rent Capping." Saving two thousand rupees on a
policy with a room limit can cost you sixty thousand rupees during a surgery through
proportionate cuts.

Rule Three: Ask about a Consumables Cover. Gloves, masks, and syringes can be five to
fifteen percent of a hospital bill. An add-on cover of five hundred to eight hundred rupees covers
them completely.

[slight smile] Remember: You pay for insurance so your family has peace of mind. Know the
five-year rule. Use the Ombudsman. And protect your hard-earned savings.

Be smart. Be informed. And always... be practical.
