## Panel's top 5 problems
1. 1991 vs today mixes two measures → say "all medicine powder India bought" then "just for antibiotic powder".
2. Reassurance comes 11 minutes late → say "your medicines are safe; this is about who controls the supply" early.
3. Jargon pile-up (penicillin G → six A.P.A.; para-aminophenol) → simplify, "remember that name"; "a raw chemical paracetamol is made from".
4. Too many numbers in a row → one number per beat, rupees not dollars.
5. Foreign phrasing and screen-assuming lines → plain Indian phrasing; "the central government", not "Delhi".

Panel's suggested first 10 s: "India is called the pharmacy of the world. It sends medicines to around two hundred
countries. / But the white powder inside your antibiotic, the part that actually cures you? There's a fair chance its
journey began in China."
