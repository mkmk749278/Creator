# Treatment: The Pharmacy of the World's Missing Powder

*India's Missing Pieces, episode 4 (after chips, rare earth magnets, jet engine). English, Akash. About 11–12 minutes.
Draft 1, 2026-10-28 (★ = recommended).*

**Owner's answers (2026-10-28):** angle: "cover all, but concentrate more on what the audience likes and should feel
proud of India", so A carries the film with B (Dolo/2020) and C (the 1991 number) inside it, and pride beats lead
(Cipla's dollar a day, Pimpri 1956, Kakinada 2024). Cold open: "same as question 2", read as both openings combined
(capsule for 0:00–0:08, then the 2020 order); to confirm. Thumbnail and title: "should be clickable, the audience should
feel curious to click", so curiosity first for both, with 3+ concepts each tested in `thumb_feed.py` and a click panel.

**Logline.** India makes the pills for half the world, but the white powder inside many of them, above all the
penicillin family, starts in China. India once made it, in a factory Nehru's government built in 1954, then lost every
plant. In 2024 a plant in Kakinada switched the fermenters back on, and since January 2026 a legal floor price has been
keeping it alive. That floor expires in January 2027. **Will the comeback survive?**

**Tension:** pride ("pharmacy of the world") against dependence (86 of every 100 rupees of antibiotic ingredients India
imports go to China), resolved as a live bet, not a lecture.

Rise (Pimpri 1956), fall (every plant stopped), comeback (Kakinada 2024), and the bet (floor price ends January 2027). | Best: a real arc with a low point, an Andhra plant for our audience, a date to watch, and one mechanism (fermentation is a game of scale) |

**Why ours is different** from "how India became pharmacy of the world" (pride films and their reactions) and the news clips
(dependence, with no mechanism): we follow **one molecule**, show **why** scale wins inside a fermenter, and end on a **date**.

## Cold opens considered
1. ★ **The paradox in motion (0:00–0:25).** Third of March, twenty twenty. A one-page Indian government order: paracetamol
   and 25 other medicine ingredients may no longer leave India without a licence. Why would the pharmacy of the world lock
   its doors? Because a province in China had just shut down. Then: "and one medicine shows it better than any other."
2. A child's fever, a strip of amoxicillin on a kitchen table; push into the capsule powder, which flies back along a map
   to a fermenter in China (ILLUSTRATION). Lovely, but slower; it becomes signature moment 1 at about 0:40.
3. Pimpri, 1956: Indian-made penicillin rolls off the line. Fails the lede rule as an opener (history first); kept for
   chapter 2.

## Shape (chapter names = search phrases)
0:00 **Pharmacy of the world, locked** (2020 order) → 0:40 **What's inside your capsule** (API glossed once; the capsule

**Feelings:** start: a jolt (the pharmacy locked its doors); low point at ~5:30: the roll call of shut plants in silence;
end: sober hope, a fermenter humming in Andhra and a date in the viewer's calendar.

## Art direction (fresh for this film)
**"Clean room."** Pharmaceutical whites and stainless steel against deep teal shadows, the cold light of a quality lab.
Colour = meaning: **mould green** = penicillin/biology, **amber** = China's supply, **saffron-white** = Indian-made,
**red stamp** = a stopped plant. Signature device: the **CAPSULE GAUGE**, a translucent capsule in the corner that fills with
the share of the powder that comes from China (fills as the VO gives numbers; labelled with the source). Texture: macro
footage of powders and crystals, blister-pack foil glints, document push-ins (DGFT orders, CAG audit pages).

## Signature moments
1. **The capsule trip** (~0:50): macro push into the powder; grains lift and fly as a particle stream across a 3D map from
   Kakinada to Shijiazhuang (ILLUSTRATION).
2. **The roll call** (~5:30): 1.5 s of silence, then factory names stamp STOPPED one by one (Alembic, Sarabhai, IDPL,
   Hindustan Antibiotics, Torrent, Ranbaxy, Standard) while the gauge climbs from under one per cent to eighty-six; a low
   thud on the last stamp.
3. **Inside the fermenter** (~7:00): a Blender cutaway of a stirred tank where *Penicillium* mould works for days; a
   person drawn to scale at its foot, so the viewer feels why the biggest tanks win.

## Sound
Lab hum, air handlers, the click of a blister pack, a pill bottle rattle; a cold piano-and-pad score that warms in the
last chapter; silence before the roll call and before the January 2027 reveal.

## Hard lines in this film
Health: no advice to buy, switch or avoid any medicine; quality of Indian or Chinese medicine is not in question (the
issue is supply, not safety). No company is a villain: name companies only from documents. SEBI lane: no stock talk
(Aurobindo is a listed company: say what the plant does, never whether to invest). All China numbers attributed (NITI
Aayog, CFR, RIS).
