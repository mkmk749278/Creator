# Changes from the owner's brief (script v1, 2026-10-13)

The brief's structure, hook, acts, title and palette are kept. The narration was rewritten into the channel's simple
Indian English (PLAYBOOK §4a-2: short sentences, everyday words, numbers as words) and these facts were changed because
the sources say otherwise or could not support them. Sources are in `../claims.csv`.

| Brief said | Script v1 says | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tejas jets "cannot fly", "sit grounded on assembly jigs" | New jets are built and waiting; **not one has joined the Air Force yet** | The Mk1A jets do fly test sorties; what hasn't happened is delivery to the IAF (HAL CMD, Oct 2026: 27 airframes built). |
| The only reason is the engine | Engine is **the biggest** hold-up; radar and weapons integration also pending | HAL's chairman lists one or two integration issues too. |
| "Eight thousand miles" | "About thirteen thousand kilometres" | Same distance (13,065 km, our calculation), in units an Indian viewer uses. |
| "Roughly thirty" squadrons | "About twenty nine" | IAF chief's 2026 briefing and The Wire: 29. |
| Only **four** nations can build a military turbofan (US, UK, France, Russia) | "Only a handful": America, Russia, Britain, France and, after decades, **China** | The brief's own Act 4 says China's WS-10/WS-15 freed it from Russia; a four-nation list contradicts that. |
| 1998: "G-E was barred from shipping engines… avionics and test software disappeared" | F404 sales stopped; American help on flight controls ended; India built its own flight controls | What the sources support; "software disappeared" had no source. |
| Kaveri launched in 1986 | 1986 decision; full development **sanctioned March 1989 at ₹382.81 crore**, due Dec 1996 | PIB (Ministry of Defence, 2012). |
| ₹382 Cr → ₹2,105 Cr; "over two thousand crore by 2008" | Estimate grew to **₹2,839 crore**; **₹1,996 crore** spent by 2012 | PIB 2012 figures. |
| Flight tests in Russia happened before the 2008 delinking | Delinked **Sept 2008**; first flight on the Il-76 test bed in **2010** (27 flights, 57 h, up to 12 km) | Order of events per PIB / Airforce Technology. |
| Re-ignition at 45,000 ft | Dropped (12 km, as PIB reports) | No source for 45,000 ft. |
| Compressor stalls that "extinguish the flame mid-flight" | Dropped | No reliable source found yet. |
| Rocket burns "three to six minutes" | "A few minutes" | Burn times differ by stage; kept general. |
| 9-G turns, idle to afterburner "in three seconds", 4,000 hours | Full power "in seconds"; "thousands of hours between overhauls" | Kept to what sources support (GE: 4,000–6,000 h overhaul intervals on the F414). |
| Combustion gas 1,650 °C vs superalloy melting point 1,455 °C | Rolls-Royce: blades work in gas **up to 200 degrees above the metal's melting point** | 1,455 °C is pure nickel; superalloys melt lower (CMSX-4 solidus ≈ 1,339 °C). Rolls-Royce's own statement says it cleanly. |
| 20,000 rpm, 20,000 g, "an elephant from a soup spoon" | **More than 12,000 rpm; the pull on one blade equals the weight of a London bus** (Rolls-Royce) | The brief's numbers had no source; Rolls-Royce's comparison does. |
| Rhenium off by 0.1% → blade warps; hole one micron off → turbine melts | Dropped; replaced by "the real knowledge is in how the metal is made, cast, drilled and tested" | Unsourced exaggeration. |
| "Blowing up hundreds of engines" | "Decades of testing, and many failures" | Same idea without an unsourced number. |
| Dry Kaveri "has found its ultimate mission powering Ghatak" | Dry Kaveri gave ~48.5 kN in Russia (target 46); **chosen** for combat drones like the **planned** Ghatak | Ghatak is not built yet; certification of the engine is still pending. |
| DMRL "certified" single-crystal HP blades (for fighters) | DMRL learned to make single-crystal blades, **first for a helicopter engine** (2021) | PIB April 2021. |
| AMCA Mk2 engine **110 kN** | **About 120 kN** class | All 2025–26 reporting gives 120 kN (110–130). |
| Safran partnership "transfers… directly onto Indian soil" (done deal) | Safran **picked in 2025**; deal **still awaiting final approval** (Oct 2026); Rolls-Royce and Reliance also offered an engine in Aug 2026 | Latest reports; the CCS approval had not been announced. |
| China "poured over forty billion dollars" | "Decades, and many failures" | No source for $40 bn. |
| "Software architects write code for supercomputers" | Dropped | Vague; no clear claim to source. |
| No channel intro | "Welcome to Be Practical with Kishore" after the 30-second hook | Channel rule: logo sting when the channel is named. |
