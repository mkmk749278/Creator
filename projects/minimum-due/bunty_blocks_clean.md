## Block 1 · 3,916 characters · lines M001–M049

A message comes on your phone. Your credit card statement is ready. Two numbers. Total amount due, thirty thousand rupees. Minimum amount due, just fifteen hundred. Salary has not come yet. Paying the minimum is enough, right? No late fee, CIBIL score is safe. Many people think like this. But this is the real trap. ICICI Bank has put an example on its own website. Look at this. One customer's bill was sixty nine thousand, six hundred and eighty eight rupees. On the due date, the customer paid sixty five thousand. That is almost the full bill. Still, the next statement had four thousand, three hundred and seventeen rupees of interest and GST. Only four thousand, six hundred and eighty eight was left unpaid. But the interest was almost the same amount. How? By the end of this video, you will understand the full calculation behind that small number. In simple words. Hello and welcome to Be Practical with Kishore.

First, those two numbers. Total amount due is the full money you owe the bank. Minimum amount due is the small part you must pay, so that you are not overdue. Usually it is around five percent of what you owe, plus fees and GST. So for a thirty thousand bill, the minimum is about fifteen hundred. Paying the minimum gives you two benefits. No late fee. And no past due report to CIBIL. But it does not stop the interest. And it does not save your free period. So remember this. Minimum amount due is not a safety net... it is the switch that turns on the interest meter.

From the day you swipe your card till the due date, the bank charges no interest. This is called the interest free period. Let's take an example. On October fifth, ten thousand for a phone. On the twelfth, fifteen thousand for a family function. On the twentieth, five thousand for groceries. On October twenty fifth, the statement comes. Total, thirty thousand. Due date, November fourteenth. So the phone money gets forty days free. The grocery money gets twenty five days free. But there is one condition. You must pay the full total amount before the due date. Even one rupee less, and the free period is gone. This is written in the RBI rules. If you do not pay in full, interest can be charged from the date of each purchase.

On November fourteenth, you paid the minimum of fifteen hundred. Now let's see what the bank does. Rule number one. Interest does not start from the due date. It starts from the day you spent the money. On the ten thousand for the phone, interest from October fifth. On the fifteen thousand, from October twelfth. On the five thousand, from October twentieth. The rate? In banks like ICICI, three point seven five percent per month. That is forty five percent per year. Rule number two. You pay interest even on the money you paid back. Till November fourteenth, interest on the full thirty thousand. Only after that, on the remaining twenty eight thousand five hundred. Rule number three. On that interest, eighteen percent GST. On November twenty fifth, the next statement comes. You paid fifteen hundred. The bank added interest and GST of one thousand, nine hundred and eighty one. So without spending a single new rupee, your balance went up from thirty thousand to thirty thousand, four hundred and eighty one. You paid, and still your debt went up. This is our calculation. But the method is the same one ICICI shows on its website. The same thing happened to the ICICI customer we saw at the start. Interest on the full bill till the payment day. Then no free days on new purchases. That is how it became four thousand, three hundred and seventeen. Rule number four. Say on November fifteenth, you fill petrol with the same card. Will that get a free period? No. Until the old balance is fully cleared, every new swipe gets interest from that same day. So from the day you paid only the minimum, your card is no longer free. It has become a loan at forty five percent interest.

Now, what if you keep paying only the minimum every month? As per RBI rules, your debt should not grow when you pay the minimum. So the debt does come down. But how slowly? In the first year, out of every hundred rupees you pay, about seventy four rupees go to interest and GST. Only twenty six rupees reduce your debt. Start with thirty thousand. After one year, you would have paid about twenty thousand. But you still owe about twenty four thousand, eight hundred. After five years? You have paid about seventy thousand. And you still owe about thirteen thousand. To clear it fully, it takes more than ten years. That is why the RBI made banks print a warning on every statement. If you pay only the minimum, repayment can go on for years.

India now has about eleven point eight six crore credit cards. As per CRIF High Mark, card dues unpaid for ninety one to three hundred and sixty days were thirty three thousand, eight hundred and eighty six crore rupees by March twenty twenty five. That is forty four percent more than the year before. Forty five percent is too high, right? But in December twenty twenty four, the Supreme Court set aside an old order that capped credit card interest at thirty percent. So today, there is no limit set by any court. So what protects you is not that small number. It is knowing the calculation.

Some people suggest a shortcut. Card rotation. That means, take money from a friend and pay the bill. The next day, take the money out of the card in the name of rent, and give it back. But your debt does not reduce by even one rupee. Only the due date moves to next month. And you pay a fee. Also, after new RBI rules in twenty twenty five, apps like PhonePe, Paytm and CRED removed the option to pay rent by credit card. Taking cash from your credit card is even more dangerous. There is a fee, and interest starts from the same day. So rotation is not a solution. It only pushes the debt forward.

So what should we do? Five simple points. First. Whenever you can, pay the total amount due in full. If you set up auto debit, set it for the total amount, not the minimum. Then your card is really free. Depending on the card, up to fifty days without interest. Second. If you cannot pay in full, pay as much as you can. But never less than the minimum. Because interest on the money you pay stops on that day. The more you pay, the less interest. And once you clear the full balance, the free period comes back. Third. While you have a balance, keep that card aside. Use UPI or a debit card for daily spending. Fourth. If the balance is big, ask your bank for an EMI option. HDFC's EMI rates are around eleven to twenty five percent. Also add the processing fee and GST. And stay away from unknown loan apps. Fifth. If your due date comes before your salary, ask the bank to change your billing cycle. As per RBI rules, they must give you this option. One simple check. Look at the finance charges line on your statement. If it is not zero, you are paying interest.

Remember. The minimum amount due protects you from late fees and a bad CIBIL report. Not from interest. Remember three numbers. Forty five percent interest. Eighteen percent GST on top. And zero free days on new spending while you have a balance. The minimum is like an emergency brake. Not a road you take every day. What do you pay every month? The total, or the minimum?
