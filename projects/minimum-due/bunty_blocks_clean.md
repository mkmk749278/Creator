## Block 1 · 3,934 characters · lines M001–M049

A message comes on your phone. Your credit card bill is ready. It shows two numbers. Total amount due, thirty thousand rupees. Minimum amount due, only fifteen hundred. Salary has not come yet. So you think, I will pay only the minimum, right? No late fee. CIBIL score is safe. Many people think like this. But this is the real trap. ICICI Bank has put one example on its own website. Look. One customer's bill was sixty nine thousand, six hundred and eighty eight rupees. On the last date, the customer paid sixty five thousand. That is almost the full bill. But in the next bill, the bank added four thousand, three hundred and seventeen rupees for interest and GST. Only four thousand, six hundred and eighty eight was not paid. But the interest was almost the same. How? By the end of this video, you will understand the full maths behind that small number. In very simple words. Hello friends, welcome to Be Practical with Kishore.

First, the two numbers. Total amount due means the full money you have to pay the bank. Minimum amount due is the small part you must pay to not be late. Most banks keep it near five percent of your bill, plus fees and GST. So for a thirty thousand bill, the minimum is about fifteen hundred. Paying the minimum does two good things. No late fee. And the bank will not report you as late to CIBIL. But it does not stop interest. And you lose your free days. So remember this. The minimum amount is not a safety net... it is a switch that starts the interest meter.

From the day you use your card till the last date to pay, the bank does not charge interest. These are your free days. Let's take an example. On October fifth, ten thousand for a phone. On the twelfth, fifteen thousand for a family function. On the twentieth, five thousand for groceries. On October twenty fifth, the bill comes. Total, thirty thousand. Last date to pay, November fourteenth. So the phone money gets forty free days. The grocery money gets twenty five free days. But there is one condition. You must pay the full bill before the last date. Even one rupee less, and all the free days are gone. This is in the RBI rules. If you do not pay the full bill, the bank can charge interest from the day you spent.

On November fourteenth, you paid only the minimum, fifteen hundred. Now let's see what the bank does. Rule number one. Interest does not start from the last date. It starts from the day you spent the money. On the ten thousand for the phone, interest from October fifth. On the fifteen thousand, from October twelfth. On the five thousand, from October twentieth. How much interest? In banks like ICICI, three point seven five percent every month. That is forty five percent in a year. Rule number two. You pay interest even on the money you already paid. Till November fourteenth, interest on the full thirty thousand. Only after that, on the balance of twenty eight thousand five hundred. Rule number three. On top of the interest, eighteen percent GST. On November twenty fifth, the next bill comes. You paid fifteen hundred. The bank added interest and GST of one thousand, nine hundred and eighty one. So without spending even one new rupee, your bill went up from thirty thousand to thirty thousand, four hundred and eighty one. You paid money, and still your loan went up. This is our own maths. But we used the same method ICICI shows on its website. The same thing happened to the ICICI customer we saw at the start. Interest on the full bill till the day of payment. Then no free days for new shopping. That is how it became four thousand, three hundred and seventeen. Rule number four. Say on November fifteenth, you fill petrol with the same card. Will you get free days for that? No. Till the old bill is fully paid, every new swipe gets interest from that same day. So from the day you paid only the minimum, your card is not free any more. It has become a loan at forty five percent interest.

## Block 2 · 3,541 characters · lines M051–M100

Now, what if you pay only the minimum every month? RBI rules say your loan should not grow if you pay the minimum. So the loan does come down. But very, very slowly. In the first year, out of every hundred rupees you pay, about seventy four rupees go to interest and GST. Only twenty six rupees cut your loan. Start with thirty thousand. After one year, you would have paid about twenty thousand. But you still owe about twenty four thousand, eight hundred. After five years? You have paid about seventy thousand. And you still owe about thirteen thousand. To fully close it, it takes more than ten years. That is why RBI makes banks print a warning on every bill. If you pay only the minimum, you will keep paying for years.

India now has about eleven point eight six crore credit cards. As per CRIF High Mark, credit card bills not paid for three months to one year were thirty three thousand, eight hundred and eighty six crore rupees, in March twenty twenty five. That is forty four percent more than the year before. Forty five percent is too much, right? But in December twenty twenty four, the Supreme Court cancelled an old order that had put a thirty percent limit on credit card interest. So today, no court has put any limit. So what saves you is not that small number. It is knowing this maths.

Some people use a shortcut. They call it card rotation. Take money from a friend and pay the full bill. The next day, take the money out of the card as rent, and give it back to the friend. But your loan does not come down even by one rupee. Only the last date moves to next month. And you pay a fee. Also, after new RBI rules in twenty twenty five, apps like PhonePe, Paytm and CRED stopped rent payment by credit card. Taking cash from your credit card is even worse. There is a fee, and interest starts from the same day. So rotation is not a solution. It only pushes the loan to next month.

So what should we do? Five simple steps. One. When you can, pay the full bill. If you set auto pay, set it for the full amount, not the minimum. Then your card is really free. Up to fifty free days, depending on your card. Two. If you cannot pay the full bill, pay as much as you can. But never less than the minimum. Because interest on the money you pay stops that day. Pay more, and you pay less interest. And once you clear the full bill, your free days come back. Three. Till the bill is cleared, keep that card aside. Use UPI or a debit card for daily spending. Four. If the bill is big, ask your bank to change it into EMI. For example, HDFC's EMI rates are about eleven to twenty five percent. Also check the fee and GST. And never take loans from unknown apps. Five. If your last date comes before your salary, ask the bank to change your bill date. RBI rules say they must give you this option. One easy check. On your bill, look for the line called finance charges. If it is not zero, you are paying interest.

Remember. The minimum amount saves you from late fees and a bad CIBIL report. Not from interest. Remember three numbers. Forty five percent interest. Eighteen percent GST on top. And zero free days for new spending till the old bill is cleared. The minimum is like an emergency brake. Use it only in an emergency, not every month. What do you pay every month?
