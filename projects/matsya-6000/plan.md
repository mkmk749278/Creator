# Plan · The 6,000-Metre Titanium Sphere (matsya-6000) · v1, 2026-11-09

## The promise (locked when the owner approves)
**Promise in one sentence:** six kilometres down, the sea will press on India's Matsya 6000 with almost a lakh tonnes,
and you'll see why the three people inside sit in a titanium ball, and why one seven-metre weld around its middle,
which ISRO's rocket welders needed about seven hundred trials to learn, decides whether they come home.

**Title (main):** **Why India's Deep-Sea Sub Is a Titanium Ball** (43 characters)
- Alt 1: The One Weld Between 3 Indians and the Sea (42)
- Alt 2: India's 6 KM Dive Hangs on One Weld (35)
*Why the main:* a question a stranger can't answer ("why a ball?"), the search words "India", "deep sea", "sub" early,
and it leaves the weld for the thumbnail, so title and picture add up instead of repeating. Search demand for
"Matsya 6000" is small (no long video over 100K) but the curiosity around deep subs and Titan is huge (17.7M Hindi
views for one Titan video); 90% of our views come from the home feed, so the package must work on curiosity, not
search.

**Thumbnail concept (main): "ONE WELD"**: our Blender render of the titanium sphere floating in black water, cut
away so three empty seats glow warm inside (no people), one thin seam around its equator glowing white-hot, faint
pressure arrows pressing in from every side. Words: **ONE WELD** in caution yellow, left; bottom-right empty.
Visual question: *what is that glowing line, and why does everything hang on it?*
- Challenger 1: **600 BAR**: the same sphere, tighter, the arrows bigger, a fingernail-sized square with "615 KG"
  (OUR CALCULATION) beside it.
- Challenger 2: **WHY A BALL?**: the whole Matsya vehicle in the abyss, its frame see-through, the ball at its heart
  lit like a lantern.
- Alt 3 (not used): **6 KM DOWN**: a tiny light far below a depth scale. Weaker: no object to wonder about.
**Promise check for sentence 1:** it must put the viewer inside the ball, name the depth and the weld. Draft:
"Imagine sitting inside this ball. It's titanium, two metres across, and six kilometres of sea are pressing on it
from every side. Only one thing holds it together. A single weld, right around its middle."

**Edge:** everyone says "India is going six kilometres down"; nobody shows the titanium ball that has to hold back
almost a lakh tonnes of sea, why ISRO's rocket welders had to make it, and why, after Titan, the whole mission rests on
one seam.

**Length:** about 12 minutes. **Feeling:** claustrophobic awe at the start, the physical "oh" of the pressure, quiet
tension through the weld, respect, and an honest wait at the end.

## 2. Cold opens considered
1. **Inside the ball (chosen).** Darkness; a porthole; three empty seats in a two-metre titanium room (H1,
   RECONSTRUCTION, no faces); the depth readout ticks past 5,900 m; the hull ticks. Sentence 1 as above. *Why:* the
   first frame is the thumbnail's subject, and the viewer is inside the moment, not watching a news item.
2. The beam: ISRO's own footage of the weld, a white line crawling round a sphere for 32 minutes (kept for ch3, the
   signature).
3. August 2025: two Indians climb out of a French submarine after five kilometres (kept for ch4; real people should
   not be a hook).

## 3. Endings considered
1. **The wait (chosen).** Back inside the ball, lights off: "Matsya hasn't been to sea with people in it yet. When it
   does, every number in this film gets tested in one dive." Then why India goes at all (the nodule field 2,000 km
   south), and the subscribe line built like the AIP film's ("When Matsya makes its first dive, we'll be there.").
2. The bookend of spheres: America's Alvin started with a steel sphere and moved to titanium in 1973; India lowered
   a steel sphere in 2021 and welded titanium in 2025 (used inside ch2 instead, if the steel-sphere test is
   confirmed).
3. The aquanauts surfacing from Nautile (weaker: it's someone else's machine).

## 6. Signature moments
- **S1 · The fingernail to the lakh tonnes:** one square centimetre, then the car, then arrows covering the whole
  ball while a counter climbs to about 98,000 tonnes. The physics clicks in ten seconds.
- **S2 · The equator:** ISRO's real footage of the beam, then the seam peels off the sphere and unrolls into a 7.1 m
  line along the floor next to a human silhouette; "this is the whole weld; it took thirty-two minutes; one pass".
- **S3 · Two shapes, same sea:** a cylinder and a sphere under identical arrows, both labelled SIMPLIFIED; no
  implosion shown, no victims, no sound effect; the report's own words.
