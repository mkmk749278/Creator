# Plan · ISRO's Hardest Weld (matsya-6000) · v2, 2026-11-09 (after the independent review, `review_plan_v1.md`)

## The promise (**locked: the owner approved plan v2, title and thumbnail concept on 2026-11-09**)
**Promise in one sentence:** the hardest thing ISRO had to make for India's first deep-sea dive wasn't a rocket part.
It was one seam around a titanium ball that three Indians will trust six kilometres down. You'll see why it's so hard,
how they test it, and what Titan got wrong that this ball has to get right.

**Title (main):** **ISRO's Hardest Weld Isn't for a Rocket** (38 characters)
- Alt 1: 3 Indians, 6 KM Down, One Weld (30)
- Alt 2: Why ISRO Built India's Deep-Sea Ball (36)
*Why:* ISRO is the strongest click word for this audience, at home and abroad. "Isn't for a rocket" is a surprise,
not fear, and uses no Titan bait. Search demand for "Matsya 6000" is small, and 90% of our views come from the home feed,
so the package works on curiosity. (v1's "Why India's Deep-Sea Sub Is a Titanium Ball" scored 6/10 in review: "reads
like a UPSC explainer".)

**Thumbnail concept (main): "6 KM DOWN"**: our Blender render. The titanium ball hangs in black water under a huge
dark column of sea, cut away so three empty seats glow warm inside (no people). A **thick**, white-hot seam runs round
its equator, readable across a living room, and a small human silhouette stands beside the ball for scale. Words: **6 KM
DOWN** in caution yellow, upper left; bottom-right empty. Visual question: *people go inside that, down there, held
together by that glowing line?*
- Challenger 1: **ISRO'S WELD**: a tight shot of the seam glowing, the beam's blue light, the ball's curve filling the
  frame, a silhouette at the edge.
- Challenger 2: **WHY A BALL?**: the whole Matsya vehicle in the abyss, its frame see-through, the ball lit like a
  lantern (the most legible on a TV).
- Not used: "ONE WELD" (6.5: "weld means nothing until you've watched"), "600 BAR" (5: viewers can't picture bar).
**Sentence 1 (promise check):** "Six kilometres down, the sea will press on this titanium ball with almost a lakh
tonnes. Three Indians will sit inside it. And the part Isro found hardest to build is the thin line around its middle."
It confirms all three variants: the depth (6 KM DOWN), ISRO and the seam (ISRO'S WELD), the ball (WHY A BALL?).

**Edge:** everyone says "India is going six kilometres down". Nobody shows the titanium ball that has to take almost a
lakh tonnes of squeeze, why a rocket agency had to learn a new weld to make it, and how it must be proved before anyone
goes down. After Titan, that last part is everything.

**Honesty rules for this film (from the review):** the seam is *the hardest part to make*, never "the only thing
holding it together" (the titanium does; hatch and penetrations matter too). The difference from Titan is certification,
a proven material, a sphere and independent inspection, not shape alone. "Almost a lakh tonnes" is total squeeze from
every side, not a weight. The weld is "about seven metres".

**Length:** about 12 minutes. **Feeling:** awe and a little fear at the start, relief at honest answers by 2:00, quiet
tension through the weld, wonder at the deep, an honest wait at the end.
