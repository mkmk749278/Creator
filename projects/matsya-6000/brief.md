# Film 2 · The 6,000-Metre Titanium Sphere: Inside Matsya 6000

- **Slug:** `matsya-6000` · Drive: `Creator/English/matsya-6000/` (playlist: "Practical India | Hard Truths, Simply
  Explained", `PLYqsLgsTPCfg`)
- **The machine:** the personnel sphere of Matsya 6000, India's first crewed deep-ocean submersible (NIOT, Ministry of
  Earth Sciences, Samudrayaan / Deep Ocean Mission): a titanium-alloy (Ti-6Al-4V ELI) ball, 2,260 mm across with an
  80 mm wall, rated for about 600 bar, made of two halves joined by one electron-beam weld that ISRO's LPSC developed.
- **The paradox (working):** three people will sit inside a ball smaller than a car, at a depth where the sea presses
  on every fingernail with the weight of a small car, and the only thing between them and the ocean is one seam that
  took about 700 trials to learn and 32 minutes to make. Titan showed the world what happens when a hull fails.
- **Who it's for:** every curious Indian (owner, 2026-11-09): people who saw "India is going 6 km down" and the Titan
  implosion clips; students who know the name Samudrayaan from exam videos but not the mechanism; families proud of
  ISRO who don't know ISRO welded a submarine.
