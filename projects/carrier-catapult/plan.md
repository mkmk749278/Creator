# Plan · India's missing catapult (carrier-catapult) · v1, 2026-11-12 (before the independent review)

## The promise (to be locked by the owner with this plan)
**Promise in one sentence:** India threw jets off a catapult in 1961, and today its carriers use a ramp; you'll see
what the ramp costs, what a modern catapult really is (a ninety-metre motor that needs a burst of power no ship can
make on the spot), and exactly how far India is from having one.

**Title (main):** **India Had a Catapult in 1961. Why Not Now?** (41 characters)
- Alt 1: Why INS Vikrant Has a Ramp, Not a Catapult (42)
- Alt 2: Rafale M Is Here. Where Is India's Catapult? (44)
*Why:* a fact every Indian can check against an expectation (the form that travelled, PLAYBOOK §4.4): India had it
65 years ago. The roadmap's "Why India Can't Build an Aircraft Carrier Catapult" is not a sourced fact (India has a
small demonstrator, no approved catapult carrier and a reported US offer since 2017), so the title asks instead of
asserting. Alt 1 is the search wording; Alt 2 rides the Rafale M news.

**Thumbnail concept (main): "NO CATAPULT".** A realistic picture, bright and warm: a fighter in full afterburner at
the foot of INS Vikrant's ramp in low golden sun, the ramp rising huge in front of it. The flat deck under the jet is
cut away to show an empty, dark slot where a catapult's glowing track should be, drawn only as a dotted outline.
Words **NO CATAPULT** in caution yellow, upper left; bottom-right empty. Visual question: *why is that jet about to
drive up a ramp, and what's missing under it?*
- Challenger 1, **1961: YES**: left half an old Sea Hawk on the first Vikrant's catapult (period colour, steam
  rising), right half today's jet at the ramp. The contradiction of the title, as a picture.
- Challenger 2, **3 SECONDS** (the bright plain-object version): a huge steel disk spinning and glowing at its rim
  below a deck, a jet above it at the moment of leaving.
- Not used: "WHY A RAMP?" (answers nothing a viewer feels), a Fujian-against-Vikrant split (race bait; dark).
- The pictures: generated from our own Blender model of the deck and from real reference photos, as the Matsya
  thumbnails were (owner, 2026-11-12); no real person, no insignia invented.

**Sentence 1 (promise check, draft):** "On the twenty-ninth of October, a Rafale touched down on INS Vikrant. It
is a jet built to be thrown into the sky by a catapult. Vikrant has no catapult. It has a ramp. And here is the
strange part: India's first carrier had one, in nineteen sixty-one."
It confirms all three variants: the jet at the ramp with no catapult (NO CATAPULT), 1961 (1961: YES); 3 SECONDS is
confirmed by 0:30 ("for three seconds").

**Edge:** everyone argues about which carrier India should build; nobody shows the Indian viewer the physical fact
under the argument: a catapult is a three-second burst of power the size of a town's, which the ship has to store,
throw and repeat every 45 seconds; even its inventor is still struggling with it, and the jet built for that throw
has just landed on India's ramp.

**Honesty rules for this film:** never "India can't"; say what exists (a demonstrator for about 400 kg), what is
asked for (two catapults, in the 15-year roadmap) and what is not approved. No number for the ramp's penalty until
one is sourced. The US offer is "reported". China's choice "by its defence ministry's account". EMALS's failures and
its tens of thousands of launches are told together. 1971 is told with the Navy's own historian, no legend.

**Length:** about 11 minutes. **Feeling:** pride, then a puzzle, a loss, a memory, awe at the machine, a sober
look, and a steady ending with something to watch for.

*The obvious answer set aside:* "INS Vishal, India's future supercarrier, will have EMALS" (every existing Indian
film). It is a wish-list; ours is about what is in steel.

## 2. Cold opens considered
1. **The moment (chosen):** the real Rafale M touchdown on Vikrant, then the camera turns from the wires to the
   ramp at the other end of the deck. First frame = the thumbnail's subject (jet, ramp).
2. **A sound:** black screen, the hiss and slam of a steam catapult shot, then "India stopped making this sound in
   nineteen eighty-nine." Strong, but the first frame would not be the thumbnail.
3. **A number made physical:** a town's lights dip for three seconds as a jet leaves. Invented scene; kept honest
   only as an illustration, so not a real moment.
4. **The viewer's home:** a ceiling fan overhead, "the machine India needs is already above your head." Charming,
   but it gives away the mechanism in sentence 1.
*Why 1:* it is real, dated, proud, and holds the contradiction in one frame. The fan is kept as the one image of
chapter 3 because there it explains; here it would answer.

## 3. Endings considered
1. **The yardstick and the date (chosen):** two things to watch: the government's decision on the next carrier, ramp
   or catapult; and the day DRDO's machine throws tonnes instead of kilos. Last image: the jet at the foot of the
   ramp, three seconds of quiet.
2. **The circle:** end on the 1961 catapult shot. Warm, but it looks back; our viewer wants the next date.
3. **The open verdict:** "is a catapult even worth it?" left to the comments. Honest, but leaves the caring viewer
   unsatisfied; it becomes the one real question after the subscribe line instead.

## 4. The creative spine
- **The one image:** *the motor in your ceiling fan, cut open and rolled out flat, ninety metres long.* That is an
  electromagnetic catapult. Sized from the real length (about 91 m), shown beside a cricket pitch (20 m) four and a
  half times over (OUR CALCULATION).
- **The saying to take home:** "A ramp costs nothing. The jet pays the bill."
- **The motif, three seconds** (picture: a thin amber bar that fills in three seconds; sound: a rising electric
  whine cut dead to silence). Returns: (1) 0:30 "for three seconds"; (2) ch1, the three seconds a jet spends on the
  ramp, alone; (3) ch3, three seconds to empty four spinning disks, forty-five to fill them; (4) ch5, three seconds
  that failed once in a few hundred times on USS Ford; (5) the end: three seconds of silence on the jet at the ramp,
  the bar empty. Each return adds a meaning: a promise, a loneliness, a mechanism, a doubt, a wait.
- **The feeling curve:** pride (open) → puzzlement (ch1 start) → loss (ch1 end) → fond surprise (ch2) → awe (ch3) →
  sober (ch4) → relief that it is hard for everyone (ch5) → steady resolve (end). No two neighbours match.
- **One device per chapter:** open: a moment turned around; ch1: the viewer in the cockpit; ch2: then and now;
  ch3: one household object followed through; ch4: a ladder (kilos to tonnes); ch5: a reversal (the inventor
  doubts); end: a confession and a yardstick.
- **Kishore beside the viewer (two moments, both the viewer's own objection):** ch4 "You might ask me, Kishore,
  America offered it. Why not just buy it?"; ch5 "Kishore, even America is going back to steam. So isn't India
  right to wait?"
- **The shared memory:** December 1971, Vikrant's Sea Hawks. **Starts and ends in the viewer's life:** the news
  clip they forwarded on 30 October; the fan above them.
- **The retell line:** "You know our carrier once had a catapult, in 1961? And the new kind is basically a ceiling
  fan motor ninety metres long that needs a town's electricity for three seconds."
- **Art direction:** warm deck light (golden sun on grey steel, sea blue) for India today; hand-tinted archive
  warmth for 1961-71; the machine chapters on the steel inspection plate with amber for stored energy and cyan for
  magnetic field. Sound palette: wind over a deck, afterburner, steam hiss and slam (1961), a rising electric whine
  (EMALS), and silence as the instrument. Ours beats the strongest three because they are American (the steam
  argument), spec read-outs, or wish-lists; none starts from India's own deck or India's own history.

## 7. Signature moments and hero shots
- **S1 (ch3, 4:30): the fan unrolls.** One continuous shot: a real fan, its coil ring, the cut, the roll-out to
  91 m beside cricket pitches. The mechanism clicks without a formula.
- **S2 (ch3, 5:50): the disks sag.** Jet above, four rotors below, the tone of the drone dropping as the jet goes.
- **S3 (open and end): the empty slot.** The camera sinks through Vikrant's deck under the jet and finds nothing.
- **Hero 3D (Blender Cycles):** H1 the deck and the empty slot (push down through steel); H2 the fan-to-track
  unroll (one move, 25 s); H3 the rotor room (slow orbit, speed tied to the launch); H4 the cockpit at the ramp and
  the closing wide. Three.js for the lip of the ramp, the steam piston and the magnetic wave.
- **Real people in every chapter:** deck crew at the landing (open), a ramp launch crew (ch1), the 1971 air crew
  (ch2), a hand on a fan regulator (ch3), DRDO engineers at the demonstrator (ch4), US and Chinese deck crews (ch5),
  Indian sailors (end).
