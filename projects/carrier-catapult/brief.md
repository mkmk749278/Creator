# Film 3 · Why India Can't Build an Aircraft Carrier Catapult (working title)

- **Slug:** `carrier-catapult` · Drive: `Creator/English/carrier-catapult/` (playlist: "Practical India | Hard Truths,
  Simply Explained", `PLYqsLgsTPCfg`)
- **The machine:** the aircraft-carrier catapult, above all the electromagnetic one (EMALS): a linear motor about
  ninety metres long under the deck that throws a loaded fighter to flying speed in two to three seconds, fed by
  energy the ship stores between launches.
- **The paradox (working):** India flew jets off a steam catapult in 1961. In 2026 both its carriers use a ramp, and
  on 29 October 2026 a Rafale Marine, a jet built to be thrown by a catapult, landed on INS Vikrant, which has none.
  The Navy's own roadmap asks for two electromagnetic catapults; DRDO's demonstrator throws about 400 kg and the Navy
  needs 30 to 40 tonnes. China fitted them in November 2025; America, which invented them, was ordered in August 2026
  to plan a return to steam.
- **Roadmap subtitle check (PLAYBOOK §1.1):** "EMALS linear induction motors, flywheel storage" holds (four disk
  alternators store the energy as spin). "Can't build" is too strong as a fact: India has not tried at full scale and
  failed; it has a small demonstrator, no approved catapult carrier, and a US export offer on the table since 2017.
  The film says what is missing and why, not "cannot".
- **Who it's for:** every curious Indian (owner, 2026-11-09): people who saw the Rafale M land on Vikrant and Fujian's
  catapult clips, and who ask "why does our carrier have a ramp?" (the top YouTube results on that question are
  11-second Shorts).
