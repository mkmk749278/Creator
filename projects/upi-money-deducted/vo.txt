Your phone says debited. The shop says nothing came.
And here's the strange part — both are telling the truth.
Your money isn't lost. It's stuck between two banks... and I'll show you when it comes back.
Think about it. You pay eight hundred and forty rupees at a kirana shop, and the speaker stays silent. Sounds familiar, right?
Here's the catch. U.P.I. handles about eighty crore payments a day. So the system in the middle, run by N.P.C.I., gives every bank fifteen seconds to reply.
Let's look at a normal payment. N.P.C.I. tells your bank, take out eight hundred and forty rupees.
Your bank does it, and replies... done.
Only then does N.P.C.I. tell the shop's bank to add it. That bank adds it, and replies... done.
One payment is two steps. Money leaves one bank first, and reaches the other bank second. Makes sense?
Let's go back to your shop. Step one worked — your money is already out. Step two starts... and the shop's bank never replies.
Maybe a network link broke. Maybe the connection timed out.
Fifteen seconds pass. N.P.C.I. can't wait forever, so it marks your payment as deemed — which simply means, not sure yet.
That's why your phone says debited. For your bank, step one is finished, but for the shop, step two never arrived.
Now notice this. The system doesn't give up. N.P.C.I. goes back to the shop's bank and asks... did the money reach the shop or not?
If yes, it's a success. If not, the shop's bank sends it back, and your bank returns every rupee.
Here's the rule from the R.B.I. Paid a shop? Your money must come back within five days. Sent it to a person? By the next day.
Miss that deadline — and the bank pays you one hundred rupees for every extra day, without you asking.
At the end of the day, your money was never lost — it was only waiting.
One tip from me. Never pay a second time while the first payment still says pending.
If the first one goes through later — you've paid twice.
