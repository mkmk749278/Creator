Imagine this. You scan the Q-R at a kirana shop and pay eight hundred and forty rupees. Your phone says... debited. But the shop's speaker stays silent, and the shopkeeper says, nothing came. You know this feeling, right?
So where did your money go? Relax, it's not lost. It's stuck between two banks, and I'll show you exactly where.
See, U-P-I handles about eighty crore payments a day. So the system in the middle, run by N-P-C-I, gives every bank just fifteen seconds to reply.
First, let's watch a normal payment. You type your pin, and N-P-C-I tells your bank, take eight hundred and forty rupees from this account.
Your bank takes it out and replies, done.
Only then does N-P-C-I tell the shop's bank, add this money. The shop's bank adds it and replies, done.
So one payment is really two steps, in two different banks. Money goes out first... and comes in second. Simple, right?
Now back to your shop. Step one worked — your money is already out. Step two starts... and the shop's bank never replies.
Maybe a network link broke, or the connection timed out.
After fifteen seconds, N-P-C-I can't keep waiting, so it puts your payment on hold. The system calls it deemed — which simply means, not sure yet.
So why does your phone say debited? Because step one finished, but step two never arrived. Both screens are telling the truth.
Then N-P-C-I goes back and asks the shop's bank, did the money reach the shop or not?
If yes, it becomes a success. If not, the shop's bank sends it back, and your bank returns your money.
So what does the R-B-I rule say? Paid a shop? Your money must come back within five days. Sent it to a person? By the next day. And if the bank is late, it pays you one hundred rupees for every extra day — without you even asking.
Let me say that once more. Shop, five days. Person, next day. Late means one hundred rupees a day.
One tip from me. Before you pay again, check the first payment's status, or you may pay twice.
