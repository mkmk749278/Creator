Your phone says debited. The shop says nothing came.
So open your U.P.I. app and look. It will show you one of three words — success, failed, or processing.
Each one means something different for your money. And the third one... that's where people panic and pay twice. I'll show you why.
Here's the catch. U.P.I. handles about eighty crore payments a day, so N.P.C.I., the system in the middle, gives every bank fifteen seconds to reply.
Let's look at what a payment really is. It's two steps — first your bank takes the money out, and then the shop's bank adds it in.
Now the three words make sense, right?
Success means both steps finished, and your money has already reached the shop's bank. Done.
Failed means the payment broke before it finished. Sometimes your money never even left, and if it did, your bank has to send it back.
I'll be honest — failed is the safe one, because you can simply try again.
Now the big one. Processing — some apps say pending.
It means a reply never came back. In your case, step one worked, so your money is out. Step two started... but the shop's bank never replied. Maybe a network link broke. Maybe the connection timed out.
After fifteen seconds, N.P.C.I. can't keep waiting, so it marks your payment as deemed — which simply means, not sure yet.
Think about it. Your money isn't lost. It's waiting between two banks, and nobody knows yet which way it will go.
So N.P.C.I. goes back and asks the shop's bank... did the money reach or not?
If yes, processing becomes success. If not, the shop's bank sends it back, and your bank returns every rupee.
And there's a deadline from the R.B.I. Paid a shop? Your money must come back within five days. Sent it to a person? By the next day.
Miss that deadline — and the bank pays you one hundred rupees for every extra day, without you asking.
So here's my one rule. Never pay again while it says processing.
If the first payment goes through later — you've paid twice.
Failed means try again. Processing means wait.
