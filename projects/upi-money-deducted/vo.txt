Here's what happens after you type your pin.
First, your bank takes the money out of your account. That's the debited message you see.
Then N.P.C.I. — the middleman that runs U.P.I. — calls the shop's bank and says, please add this money to the shop's account.
I like to think of it as a phone call. And like every phone call in India... three things can happen.
One. The shop's bank picks up and says, done, the shop got it. That's success.
Two. The shop's bank picks up and says, sorry, we can't take this, maybe there's a problem with the account. That's failed.
Failed sounds scary, but it's actually the clear answer. The payment didn't happen. You can pay again, and the old money comes back on its own. Simple so far, right?
Three. The troublemaker. N.P.C.I. calls... and the line goes quiet. Hello?... Hello?... Call dropped.
Did the shop's bank add the money before the call dropped? Nobody knows yet. Your app shows this as pending, or processing.
So right there at the counter, don't pay again. Ask the shopkeeper to check their bank S.M.S., not the soundbox.
Bank says received? The shop got it. You're done. Nothing yet? Then it's still processing — and your money will either reach the shop, or come back to you.
N.P.C.I. waits fifteen seconds. No answer? It doesn't cancel. It keeps your payment on hold, and the banks sort it out later.
So where is your money right now? It's out of your account, and it's with the banks — not lost.
Now the shop's bank has to answer one simple question — did it go into the shop's account, or not? Like a teacher taking attendance, it can only say yes or no.
Yes? Then the shop has your money, even though the soundbox stayed silent.
No? Then the shop's bank sends it back to your bank, and your bank puts it in your account.
How long can this take? Failed or processing, the R.B.I. has set a deadline to fix it. Paid a person? Back by the next day. Paid a shop or a business? Within five days.
And if the bank takes longer, it has to pay you one hundred rupees for every extra day. You don't even have to complain. The bank has to pay it on its own. That's the rule.
Quick summary, the way I remember it. Success, the shop got it. Failed, the payment didn't happen, so pay again. Processing, wait — up to a day for a person, up to five days for a shop.
My one rule: never pay again while it says processing. Because if that first payment turns out to be a yes... congrats, you just paid eight hundred and forty rupees... twice.
Still stuck? Open your app, go to your payment history, tap that payment, and look for U.P.I. Help. You can raise it right away. Paid twice by mistake? Choose duplicate payment there.
Still not solved? Complain to your bank. And if your bank doesn't fix it, go to the R.B.I. Ombudsman — that's the R.B.I.'s own complaint office for bank problems.
