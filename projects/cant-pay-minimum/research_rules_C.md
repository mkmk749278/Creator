# Research C: set-off law, settlement practice, borrower-wise NPA (India, checked 2026-10-12)

Card-terms set-off/cross-default clauses of HDFC and ICICI: see research_practical.md §8 (not repeated here).
Format: statement | verdict | URL + date + exact quote.

## C1. Banker's right of set-off / general lien, and debits from savings/salary accounts


**C1.1 Section 171 text (the "banker's general lien").** TRUE (text).
Indian Contract Act 1872, s.171: "Bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys of a High Court and policy-brokers may, in the
absence of a contract to the contrary, retain as a security for a general balance of account, any goods bailed to them;
but no other persons have a right to retain, as a security for such balance, goods bailed to them, unless there is an
express contract to that effect." https://indiankanoon.org/doc/903901/ (opened 2026-10-12). Plain words: the law itself
speaks of "goods"; courts extend it to money in your account (C1.2).

**C1.2 Courts extend the lien to money in a salary account; Section 60 CPC does NOT stop a bank freezing/adjusting it.**
TRUE (Kerala HC Division Bench, 9 Feb 2026), but note: term loan + guarantee, NOT a credit card; it was a freeze of a
guarantor's salary account backed by a signed guarantee clause.
Canara Bank v. Agi Kumar S, W.A. 3077 & 3176 of 2025, 2026:KER:11262, https://indiankanoon.org/doc/98197117/ (opened
2026-10-12). Single Judge had limited the lien "to the extent and period permissible under Section 60(1)(i) CPC" (para 4);
the Division Bench set that aside. Quotes: para 19 "the general lien of the bankers was extended even to the money in the
hands of the Bank, deposited by the customer."; para 19 "entitled to exercise their right of lien over the salary account
of the 1st petitioner"; para 20 "the action initiated by the respondents by freezing the account of the 1st petitioner is
not an attachment"; Section 60 CPC is "applicable only to property liable to attachment and sale in execution of a decree".
Para 16 calls the lien "a right akin to the right of set-off". So the earlier INFERENCE (Section 60 CPC protects salary
only from court attachment, not from the bank's own lien/set-off) is now SOURCED for Kerala HC; other High Courts may
differ (we did not find a Supreme Court ruling on card dues + salary).

**C1.3 Condition: same person, same capacity.** TRUE (Madras HC 8 Apr 2025, loan case, not a card).
Balaji S v. Banking Ombudsman & IndusInd Bank, W.P. 17121 of 2024, https://indiankanoon.org/doc/20670918/ (opened
2026-10-12): "the Bankers right of lien cannot be exercised in case the other loan is not by the same person" (para 8.9);
lien "cannot be exercised in respect of the outstanding balance account of some other customer" (para 8.6). Plain words:
the bank can use YOUR money for YOUR debt; not a family member's account (unless they signed as guarantor/co-borrower,
as in C1.2), and not a joint account held in a different capacity (INFERENCE from "same capacity" line of cases; not opened).

**C1.4 Credit card dues taken from a savings account at the same bank: national commission sided with the bank.**
PARTLY (news report of an NCDRC order; primary order not found).
GoodReturns, 20 Nov 2019, https://www.goodreturns.in/news/hdfc-bank-credit-card-customer-your-dues-can-be-settled-fro-1102814.html
(opened 2026-10-12): HDFC Bank v. Anish Munjal (ex-Centurion Bank of Punjab account); card clause-6: "The bank, at any time
and without notice, will have lien and right to set-off on all monies belonging to the Cardmember"; NCDRC held "the
petitioner bank was entitled to set off the credit card dues at any point of time", upholding a Rs 53,043.68 debit from the
savings account and setting aside the lower forums' orders for the customer. Case number/date of the order not given.
Counter-examples are on different facts (Ludhiana/Kasaragod commissions: bank charges deducted without notice; search
snippets only, not card set-off) so: UNVERIFIED that any consumer forum has stopped a card set-off where the clause exists.


**C1.5 Bank code: lien/set-off on card default only on money "in the same capacity", with notice within three days.**
TRUE (voluntary industry code, as published by IndusInd Bank; edition January 2018).
BCSBI Code of Bank's Commitment to Customers, Jan 2018, para 8.17.5 (Credit Card) (j):
https://bank.indusind.bank.in/content/dam/regulatoryDisclosure/codeOfCommitment/coc_english.pdf (opened 2026-10-12):
"We will have right to place a lien and right to set off on all monies belonging to you, being the card holder, standing to
your credit, in the same capacity, in any account whatsoever with the Bank ... if you are in default to us in payment of our
dues. We will send you intimation to this effect by e-mail on your registered e-mail id / registered mobile number or letter
within three days from placing lien / hold on your deposit account." Note: notice comes AFTER the hold (within 3 days),
not before. It is a voluntary code adopted by member banks, not an RBI regulation; whether each bank still follows this
edition: UNVERIFIED.

**C1.6 Verdict on the owner's line "the bank can sweep your entire salary".** PARTLY.
- TRUE that a bank where you hold BOTH the card and the salary/savings account can freeze or debit that account for card
  dues under its card terms (HDFC clause, §8b.3 of research_practical; NCDRC upheld an HDFC debit, C1.4) and that courts
  treat this as lien/set-off, not court attachment, so Section 60 CPC's salary protection does not apply (Kerala HC
  DB 2026, C1.2; loan case, applied by analogy to cards = INFERENCE).
- Limits: only money of the same person in the same capacity (C1.3, C1.5); only at the SAME bank (another bank cannot
  touch your account without a court order: INFERENCE from s.171 "goods bailed to them" and the same-person rule); only up
  to the dues; notice within 3 days under the code (C1.5).
- SBI Card is a separate company from SBI the bank, so an SBI salary account is not covered by SBI Card's set-off (entity
  INFERENCE, see §8b.3).
- "Entire salary": only if the dues are as large as the balance. Say "the bank can take money from your salary account at
  the same bank, up to what you owe", not "your entire salary every month".

## C2. One-time settlement for cards in practice


**C2.1 A settlement is an RBI-recognised deal: the lender accepts cash to "fully settle" its claim, approved by a higher
officer, and the lender may not lend you again for at least 12 months.** TRUE.
RBI circular RBI/2023-24/40, "Framework for Compromise Settlements and Technical Write-offs", 8 June 2023,
https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12513 (opened 2026-10-12). Annex para 1: compromise settlement
is "any negotiated arrangement with the borrower to fully settle the claims of the RE against the borrower in cash";
para 6(i): "delegation of power for such approvals rests with an authority" (at least one level above the sanctioning
authority, per the para); para 11(i): "The cooling period in respect of exposures other than farm credit exposures shall
be subject to a floor of 12 months." Plain words: after settling, that same bank/NBFC will not give you a fresh loan or
card for at least a year (the cooling period is the lender's; other lenders are not bound by it: INFERENCE from "RE").
The circular has no paragraph on how a settlement is reported to CIBIL (checked).

**C2.2 Get the settlement in writing from the bank's official channel before paying; pay only to the bank, never cash to
an agent.** PARTLY sourced (good practice; no HDFC/ICICI/Axis/SBI Card page found that states it).
- Indian Bank recovery-agent empanelment code (search snippet; PDF blocked when opened): "The borrowers/ guarantors will
  be asked to make all payment towards recovery directly to the branches where the loan account is maintained against
  valid receipts and acknowledgement. No amount in cash or in any manner will be collected from the borrowers /
  guarantors." https://www.indianbank.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/APPLICATION-FOR-EMPANELMENT-OF-RECOVERY-AGENT-BY-BANK-2.pdf
- Why the letter matters: RBI para 6 above means a settlement needs approval by a named authority, so an agent's phone
  promise is not a sanctioned settlement (INFERENCE from C2.1).
- Real dispute (already in research_practical §5.4, PARTLY): Axis Bank accepted Rs 51,000 then demanded Rs 17,989 more.
- SBI Card's payment page is the only channel its FAQ points to: "To make payment, please CLICK HERE"
  (https://www.sbicard.com/en/faq/card-blocking-unblocking.page, opened 2026-10-12).
Safe line: "Get the offer on the bank's letterhead or from its official email, and pay only into the bank's own payment
channel, never cash to an agent." Present as advice, not as a quoted rule.

**C2.3 'Settled' vs 'closed', and converting settled to clean later.** TRUE (SBI Card) / PARTLY (general).
- SBI Card FAQ, same URL, opened 2026-10-12, heading "CIBIL status clean up if account is settled:": "In case your account
  is settled, please call us to know the process and the amount payable for CIBIL status clean up." And for written-off
  accounts: "In case your account is written-off and you pay complete outstanding as per the agreement, we will process
  your request for CIBIL status clean-up which may increase your future chances to get credit."
- Experian India (research_practical §5.2): "settled" hurts; "closed" with all payments made is positive.
- General claim "pay the waived amount + get NOC, then status becomes closed": only blogs/lender explainers
  (RBL blog via search snippet: pay "the remaining or outstanding amount of your loan and procure an NOC"; getbelong.com
  summarising ICICI Home Finance). Not a TransUnion CIBIL statement. So: "the bank may update it to closed if you later
  pay what was waived; ask the bank how much" (SBI Card says "call us to know ... the amount payable"). Exact amount
  (waived principal only vs with interest) UNVERIFIED and bank-specific.
- How long 'settled' stays visible ("up to seven years"): UNVERIFIED (lender blog snippet only). Do not say a number.

**C2.4 Waiver percentages.** UNVERIFIED (see research_practical §5.1: only settlement-firm marketing). Do not put any % on
screen as fact.

## C3. Multiple cards at the same bank: borrower-wise NPA


**C3.1 The rule text: NPA status is per borrower, not per account; a card is NPA when the minimum due is unpaid 90 days.**
TRUE.
- RBI Master Circular IRAC, RBI/2025-26/13, 1 Apr 2025, https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasCirculardetails.aspx?id=12822
  (fetched 2026-10-12). Para 4.2.7 heading "Asset Classification to be borrower-wise and not facility-wise"; 4.2.7.1: "It is
  difficult to envisage a situation when only one facility to a borrower/one investment in any of the securities issued by
  the borrower becomes a problem credit/investment and not others. Therefore, all the facilities granted by a bank to a
  borrower and investment in all the securities issued by the borrower will have to be treated as NPA/NPI and not the
  particular facility/investment or part thereof which has become irregular." Para 4.2.19.2: "A credit card account will be
  treated as non-performing asset if the minimum amount due, as mentioned in the statement, is not paid fully within 90
  days from the payment due date mentioned in the statement."
- Exceptions listed in 4.2.7 are only bills discounted under LCs (4.2.7.3), certain 2007-08 FX derivatives (4.2.7.4) and
  PACS/FSS on-lending (4.2.10). No exception for retail loans or credit cards (checked the full text by keyword).
- Current text: the Master Circular has been replaced by RBI (Commercial Banks – Income Recognition, Asset Classification
  and Provisioning) Directions, 2025, 28 Nov 2025, RBI/DOR/2025-26/164, listed on RBI's site "Updated as on October 01,
  2026" (https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13146, opened 2026-10-12; the PDF itself is behind a
  captcha). Text via TaxGuru reproduction (https://taxguru.in/?p=1005741, opened 2026-10-12; PARTLY, not the RBI copy):
  para 44 "Asset classification shall be borrower-wise and not facility-wise."; para 42(10) card NPA rule as above;
  para 71: "In case of borrowers having more than one credit facility from a bank, loan accounts shall be upgraded from NPA
  to standard asset category only upon repayment of entire arrears of interest and principal pertaining to all the credit
  facilities."
- Coming change: RBI press release 27 Apr 2026 says new final "Commercial Banks – Asset Classification, Provisioning and
  Income Recognition" Directions "shall come into effect from April 01, 2027"
  (https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=62628, opened 2026-10-12). Whether the borrower-wise
  rule is unchanged there: UNVERIFIED (not opened). Fine for an Oct 2026 film.

**C3.2 Do banks apply it to retail customers? A bank's own customer FAQ says yes.** TRUE (IndusInd, for overdraft/cash
credit customers; applies the same RBI rule; not a card example).
IndusInd Bank, "FAQs on RBI guidelines on classification of CC/OD Accounts as Non-Performing Assets (NPA)",
https://www.indusind.bank.in/content/dam/indusind-corporate/Other/FAQ-OD-CC-NPA.pdf (opened 2026-10-12, undated, cites
RBI circulars of 12 Nov 2021 and 15 Feb 2022): Q4 "the NPA classification has to be done at the borrower level and not
facility wise. Therefore, if your CC/OD account is classified as NPA, any other facility granted by the Bank will also be
classified as NPA and reported accordingly to the Credit Bureaus." Q5: upgrade "only upon repayment of entire overdue
amount of interest and principal pertaining to all the credit facilities." Q3: on NPA "all permissible withdrawals will be
suspended / frozen".
No RBI FAQ or clarification found that addresses credit cards + home/personal loan at the same bank specifically
(UNVERIFIED). INFERENCE (strong, from rule text with no retail exception): if your card at Bank X turns NPA (90 days of
unpaid minimum), your other loans at Bank X (personal, car, home) are also classified NPA in the bank's books and in
bureau reporting, even if you pay their EMIs; to fix it you must clear the arrears on all of them.
Scope limits: applies to banks. SBI Card is an NBFC (SBICPSL) under separate NBFC norms (not checked), and SBI Card and SBI
the bank are different lenders, so this rule does not tie an SBI Card default to an SBI home loan (INFERENCE from entity).
Two cards at the same bank are both "facilities" of one borrower, so both go NPA together (INFERENCE from para 44).

## Safe to say / Do not say

SAFE TO SAY
- "If your salary or savings account is in the same bank as the card, the card terms let the bank hold or take money from
  it to cover what you owe." (HDFC/ICICI clauses §8b.3; NCDRC upheld HDFC debit, C1.4; Kerala HC 2026 lien on salary
  account, C1.2.)
- "Courts have said this is the bank's own right of set-off, not a court attachment, so the usual salary protection in
  court cases does not stop it." Attribute: [KERALA HIGH COURT (2026) | CANARA BANK v AGI KUMAR] (loan case).
- "It can only take YOUR money for YOUR debt, and only in the bank where you owe." (C1.3, C1.5)
- "Under the banks' own code, they must tell you within three days of putting a hold on your account." [BCSBI CODE (2018)]
- "Banks treat a card as a bad loan, an NPA, when the minimum due stays unpaid for 90 days." [RBI IRAC RULES]
- "Once one account with a bank is an NPA, every loan you have with that same bank is marked NPA too; to clean it you must
  clear the overdue on all of them." [RBI IRAC | BORROWER-WISE RULE] (rule text; retail application per IndusInd FAQ)
- "A settlement is a written deal with the bank. Get the letter first. Pay only through the bank's own payment channel,
  never cash to an agent." (advice; C2.2)
- "After a settlement, that bank will not lend you again for at least a year." [RBI (2023) | COMPROMISE SETTLEMENT FRAMEWORK]
- "'Settled' on your credit report hurts; 'closed' is clean. Some banks will clean it up if you later pay what they waived;
  ask the bank for the amount." (SBI Card FAQ; Experian)

DO NOT SAY
- "The bank can sweep your entire salary every month." Say instead: "up to what you owe, from your account at the same bank".
- That a DIFFERENT bank (where you don't owe) can touch your account without a court order.
- That SBI Card can take money from an SBI savings account, or that an SBI Card default makes an SBI home loan an NPA
  (different companies; NBFC norms not checked).
- That set-off needs advance notice by law (code says intimation within 3 days AFTER the hold; no statute found requiring
  prior notice).
- Any settlement waiver percentage, or "settled stays seven years", or that paying the waived amount always turns
  'settled' into 'closed'.
- That Section 60 CPC protects salary from the bank (Kerala HC DB rejected this, 2026).
- That the 2027 RBI directions keep or change the borrower-wise rule (not checked).
