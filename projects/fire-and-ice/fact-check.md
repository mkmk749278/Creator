# Fire and Ice: fact-check

Independent fact-check, 8 Oct 2026 (fresh session, max effort). Every finding below comes from a page fetched today.
`claims.csv` has 56 rows with the source link and a word-for-word quote for each; all quotes were machine-checked
against the fetched pages. Times are seconds into each VO part (P1 hook, P2 Tummo, P3 Wim Hof).

## The short version

- **56 checks:** 32 fine, 5 fine with an on-screen qualifier, 1 "claim", **17 wrong, 1 unverified.** The gate
  (`scripts/claims_check.py`) fails until those 18 lines change.
- **The big errors:**
  1. Wim Hof was injected in **2011**, not 2014. In 2014 they injected **24 men**, 12 of them trained by Hof. The
     injection was a **dead bacterial fragment**, not "dangerous bacteria", and it was not "a severe infection". His
     reaction was cut by **about half**, not "completely", and not by "internal heat".
  2. Guinness says **18** titles, not "more than 26". The 26 is his own count of all records.
  3. Benson went in **February 1981** (the paper came out in January 1982), to **Upper Dharamsala**, not Ladakh.
  4. Fingers and toes warmed by **up to 8.3 °C (about 15 °F)**, not "8 to 17 °F".
  5. "Breathing ignites brown fat" is **not** what Wim Hof's scans showed. His brown fat was ordinary, and the extra
     heat came from his **breathing muscles**. Cold, not breathing, switches brown fat on.
  6. In the hook, "dead in half an hour" is the figure for **icy water**. In air it takes hours (a model gives about
     2.5 h for a naked man at −20 °C in still air). There is no "absolute rule".
  7. The papers never said "undeniable proof". That quote is invented.
- **Lines to re-record:** P1 at 20.9, 26.9 (or cut it), 55.4 and 75.8 s. P2 at 6.7, 25.0, 36.7, 54.2, 67.8, 74.6, 77.6
  and 84.8–94.9 s, which is most of P2's second half, so it's easier to redo P2 as one block. P3 at 6.9, 16.5–25.5, 35.8,
  71.5–79.3 and 102.7 s. The Telugu for each fix is below.
- **If you keep a line:** use the fallback given for it. Never put a number on screen that contradicts the VO. Leave

## Keep, with a qualifier (disputed or claim)

- **P1 15.5 s, "twenty minutes దాటితే … hypothermia వస్తుంది".** Estimates vary. An ER doctor says hypothermia "can set
  in in about 10 minutes" at −30 °F (−34 °C). A model gives about 2.5 h before core temperature falls to 30 °C for a
  naked man at −20 °C in still air. Tag the hook timeline "DRAMATISED · TIMES VARY", and don't present a minute counter
  as fact. If you re-record P1 anyway, a safer line is "twenty minutes లోనే hypothermia మొదలయ్యే ప్రమాదం ఉంది".
- **P2 61.6 s, "సెంట్రల్ హీటింగ్ సిస్టమ్ లాంటి వేడి".** Benson (1981) found **no** rise in core (rectal) temperature:
  the warm fingers came from more blood flow. But Kozhevnikov (2013) measured core (armpit) rises to 37.6 °C on
  average and 38.3 °C at most with forceful Tummo breathing. Keep it as a metaphor with an "ILLUSTRATION" tag, and show
  no core number on this line.
- **P2 71.0 s, "ఐస్ వాటర్ లో ముంచిన".** The filmed test used water at 49 °F (9 °C) in a 40 °F (4 °C) room (Gazette).
  Tricycle calls it "freezing cold water". Show no water temperature; "ROOM 40 °F (4 °C)" is safe to show. If you
  re-record P2, say "చల్లటి నీటిలో".
- **P3 0.0 s, "సూపర్ హ్యూమన్ లా ప్రూవ్ చేసిన".** Radboud said one man's result "cannot serve as scientific evidence".
  The 2014 PNAS trial (24 men) did "demonstrate" that voluntary adrenaline release damped the immune response. Hof
  himself says: "What I am capable of, everybody can learn" (GWR). Don't put "PROVED" or "SUPERHUMAN" on screen.
- **P3 47.3 s, "సీక్రెట్ … Brown Fat".** Brown fat is a real heater, but in Hof the extra heat came mainly from his
  breathing muscles. Present brown fat as one of the body's heaters. Optional new line after 87.3 s: "కానీ Wim Hof
  స్కాన్‌లలో ఆయన Brown Fat మామూలుగానే ఉంది; అదనపు వేడి ఆయన శ్వాస కండరాల నుంచి వచ్చింది!" (But in Wim Hof's scans his
  brown fat was ordinary; the extra heat came from his breathing muscles!)
- **Description only:** about a dozen drownings have been **linked** to Hof's breathing exercises. Say "linked" or
  "reported": in July 2024 a California court acquitted Hof for lack of proof.

## Fine as is (with screen tips)

- **P1 11.7 s:** blood vessels constrict within 5–10 min. True; it actually starts almost at once.

## On-screen numbers (use exactly these)

**Cold and hypothermia**
- Hypothermia = core body temperature **below 35 °C** (Paal et al. 2022).
- Stages: mild **35–32 °C** (shivering), moderate **below 32–28 °C**, severe **below 28 °C**. In young healthy adults
  the heart can stop **below about 30 °C** (Paal et al. 2022).
- Survival model, naked man in still air: **−20 °C ≈ 2.5 h**, −30 °C ≈ 1.8 h, 0 °C ≈ 9 h (Tikuisis 1995). Label it
  "MODEL ESTIMATE".
- Cold water below 15 °C: the heart can stop after **about 30 min** (Paal et al. 2022).

**Benson and Harvard**
- **DR. HERBERT BENSON · HARVARD MEDICAL SCHOOL** (cardiologist; died 2022).
- **FEB 1981 · UPPER DHARAMSALA, INDIA · 3 MONKS** (aged 46–59) · published **NATURE, 21 JAN 1982**.
- Two monks were tested in unheated stone hermitages at **1,800–2,800 m**, one in a hotel room.
- Fingers **+5.4 °C on average (9.8 °F)**. Toes **+6.4 °C on average (11.6 °F)**. Biggest rise **+8.3 °C (≈15 °F)**, in
  one monk's toe. Navel and lower back **+1.9 °C**. Core (rectal): **no change**.
- Finger and toe temperatures rose from about **22 °C to 33 °C**, still below normal body temperature.
- Wet-sheet test (a monastery in northern India; Benson's team filmed the ceremony in 1985): room **40 °F (4 °C)**;
  **3 × 6 ft** sheets soaked in **49 °F (9 °C)** water; each sheet dried in **about an hour**; **three sheets** per monk
  over several hours.
- 1985 sleep-out: February full-moon night, rocky ledge at **15,000 ft (≈4,570 m)**, **0 °F (−18 °C)**, wool or cotton
  shawls.
- February 1988, Rumtek monastery, **Sikkim**: resting metabolism lowered by up to **64%** (Benson 1990).
- Harvard Gazette sub-headline, 18 April 2002 (verbatim): **"Mind controls body in extreme experiments"**.

**Kozhevnikov 2013** (PLOS ONE, National University of Singapore)
- **10 expert meditators** (7 women), Nangchen, Qinghai (**eastern Tibet**), above 4,200 m, in rooms at about 0 °C in
  January.
- Core (armpit) temperature with forceful-breath Tummo: **37.6 °C on average, 38.3 °C at most** (largest rise 2.2 °C).
  Gentle breath: **no rise**.
- **11 Western non-meditators**, vase breathing only: **37.0 °C at most** (normal range).
- Gebchak nunnery ceremony: practitioners walk for **a few hours at −25 to −30 °C** in short skirts, with a wet sheet.

**Wim Hof**
- **WIM HOF · "THE ICEMAN" · THE NETHERLANDS** (born 20 April 1959).
- **18 Guinness World Records titles** (GWR). **26 world records** by his own count.
- Longest full-body contact with ice: **1 h 53 min 02 s (2013)**, "nearly two hours" (GWR). Others have since beaten it.
- Barefoot half marathon on ice and snow: **2 h 16 min 34 s (2007)**. Since beaten, so don't call it a current record.

**Radboud endotoxin studies** (Nijmegen, Netherlands)
- **2011, Hof himself:** endotoxin **2 ng/kg** into a vein; immune response **~50% lower** than in other volunteers;
  **hardly any flu-like symptoms**; cortisol higher than in any of 112 earlier volunteers. Paper: Psychosomatic
  Medicine, June 2012.
- **2014 (PNAS, published 5 May 2014):** **24 healthy Dutch men**: **12 trained for 10 days** by Hof (4 of those days in
  Poland) and **12 untrained**. Untrained fever: **+1.9 °C** on average at peak. Trained: TNF-α **−53%**, IL-6 **−57%**,
  IL-8 **−51%**, IL-10 **+194%**, flu-like symptoms **−56%**; adrenaline already up before the injection.

**Hof's body in the cold**
- **88 min in crushed ice up to the shoulders:** core (gut) temperature **37.7 → 37.0 °C (−0.7 °C)**, no shivering,
  heat production roughly doubled, **~170 → ~320 W** (Bongers et al. 2019, Radboud).
- Brown-fat scans: in the twin study at 13 °C his brown fat was **similar to his twin brother's**, within the normal range
  for young men (Vosselman 2014). In 2018 his brown fat activation was **"unremarkable"**, and his breathing muscles made
  the heat (Muzik 2018).

**Brown fat**
- The brown colour comes from **mitochondria rich in iron** (Cleveland Clinic; PBS).
- **UCP1** makes fuel-burning produce heat instead of ATP (Vosselman 2014; Wankhade 2016).
- **Non-shivering thermogenesis** = making heat without shivering, mainly in brown fat. It is switched on by **cold**,
  through the nerves (noradrenaline).
- There are **three** kinds of fat: white, brown and **beige** (a mix). "Two kinds" is fine for narration.
