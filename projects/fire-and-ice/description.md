How can the body make heat in freezing cold? Tummo meditation, the Harvard monk studies, Wim Hof, and brown fat.

⚠️ Safety: cold exposure and breath-holding can kill. Never practise breathing exercises in or near water (you can pass
out and drown). Ask a doctor before ice baths, especially with a heart condition.

## Facts, corrections and sources
- Hypothermia: core body temperature below 35 °C; the heart can stop below about 30 °C. How fast this happens varies a
  lot: a survival model estimates about 2.5 hours for an unclothed person at −20 °C in still air (wind and wet make it
  much faster); about 30 minutes is the figure for cold water (Paal et al. 2022; Tikuisis 1995).
- Benson et al., Nature 295:234 (published 21 Jan 1982): fieldwork in Upper Dharamsala, India, in February 1981, with 3
  monks. Fingers and toes warmed by up to 8.3 °C (about 15 °F; one monk's toe; average +5.4 °C fingers, +6.4 °C toes);
  core temperature did not change. The wet-sheet test is the monks' own tradition, filmed by Benson's team in 1985; each
  sheet dried in about an hour (Harvard Gazette, 18 Apr 2002).
- Tummo is about a thousand years old (Six Dharmas of Naropa, 11th century); Sanskrit caṇḍālī.
- Kozhevnikov et al., PLOS ONE 2013: core temperature up to 38.3 °C with forceful Tummo ("vase") breathing.
- Wim Hof: 18 Guinness World Records titles (26 world records by his own count); nearly two hours in ice (GWR, 2013).
- Radboud UMC: Wim Hof's own endotoxin test was in 2011 (immune response about 50% lower, hardly any flu-like symptoms;
  Kox et al. 2012). In 2014 (Kox et al., PNAS) 24 volunteers were injected, 12 of them trained by Hof. Endotoxin is a
  dead fragment of the E. coli cell wall; it causes a few hours of flu-like symptoms, not an infection.
- Brown fat is switched on by cold, through the nerves. Scans of Wim Hof found his brown fat ordinary; his extra heat
  came mainly from his breathing muscles (Vosselman et al. 2014; Muzik et al. 2018).
- The evidence is small: 3 monks, 10 meditators, one man, 24 volunteers. A 2024 systematic review rated the Wim Hof
  evidence "very low" quality (Almahayni & Hammond, PLOS ONE 2024). Skeptic view: McGill Office for Science and Society.
- About a dozen drownings have been linked to Hof-style breathing done in water (a 2024 court acquitted Hof).
