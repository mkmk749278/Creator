Two in the afternoon. Full sun on your rooftop solar panels... and suddenly, a power cut.

The sun is still shining. But your fans stop. Your solar isn't broken — it switched off on purpose.

Why would it quit exactly when you need it? I'll show you why, where that sunlight goes, and what keeps your fans running.

First, which box are we talking about? Not the battery inverter many of us keep at home for power cuts. I mean the solar box on your wall... the one connected to your panels.

Your panels give one-way current. Your house runs on current that swings back and forth — fifty times a second. The solar box turns one into the other.

But it can't make that swing by itself. Think of the grid as a drummer... and the solar box as a dancer that only follows the drum.

On a normal day, it follows every beat. Your house takes what it needs, and the extra goes out through your meter... to the street wire, and the transformer on the pole.

Now the electricity board cuts your line.

Here's the catch. If your solar box kept pushing, that dead wire would be alive again.

And the transformer would take your two hundred thirty volts... and push it up to about eleven thousand volts. On a wire the lineman thinks is dead.

So the solar box is built to notice. Beat missing, voltage drifting? It switches itself off — click. The safety rules give it two seconds, at most.

But sometimes your house uses exactly what your panels make. Then nothing seems to change, right? So the solar box plays a small trick. All the time, it pushes its beat slightly off... on purpose.

With the grid there, the grid pulls it back.

With the grid gone, nobody pulls it back. The beat drifts away... and the box switches off.

So what happens to your panels? The box has switched itself off, so the circuit is broken. Current has nowhere to go... so no power comes out.

The sunlight still lands. It just turns into heat, and your panels are built to sit in the sun like that.

So how do you keep your fans running? You need a different box — a hybrid inverter, with a battery.

When the grid dies, a switch inside it first cuts your house off from the street. Then the battery becomes the drummer, and the panels dance again.

Here's the rule. A solar box without a battery must go silent when the grid goes.

If anyone removes this safety switch... the lineman fixing your street takes the shock.

Your solar never failed. It just lost its drummer.
