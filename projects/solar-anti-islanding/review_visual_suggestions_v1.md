# Independent visual suggestions, round 1 (before planning the picture)

No-repo session `session_01QBFmxeJep8DtEMmaHwg4kW` (archived), 2026-10-21. Owner's ask: "take one independent
suggestion for visuals: what the audience actually likes to watch for this".

**Panel:** Priya (21), Ramesh (44, kirana, has a home inverter), Lakshmi (58, safety), Arjun (30, engineer),
Fatima (26, noisy bus, sound low) and Meera (35, motion designer).

**Inputs:** the timed narration of the final edit (`align_take2/edit_lines_exact.json`) and our hard limits only: faceless,
no fake people or events, text ≤ 15%, always moving, semantic lock. It was not told our blueprint look, so its art
direction is independent.

## What each viewer wants
- **Priya:** a real change every 2–3 s. The first 5 s should look like *her* afternoon. She wants the off-beat test played like a game. She'd swipe on a logo intro, stock thumbs-up families, or any hold over 4 s.
- **Ramesh:** show *his* inverter at 0:21 (the box with the tall tubular battery under the stairs, and its beep), then push it aside. He needs Indian homes: a flat RCC roof, a black water tank, a clothes line. He'd share the hybrid ending.
- **Lakshmi:** a calm, respectful lineman in a helmet and gloves. Never show a shock (sparks on a body, a falling person) or a fear-bait thumbnail. She'd share the "say no" moment with a hand-stop gesture.
- **Arjun:** correct physics.
  - DC is a flat line; AC electrons jiggle in place, not a river.
  - The off-beat test is shown as a small frequency drift with a number.
  - The step-up goes LT → HT, and 50 Hz is shown slowed down, with a label.
  - He'd distrust a sine wave on the DC wire, current from the panels after the box is off, an analogue meter spinning backwards, or a toy transformer.
- **Fatima:** the story must work with the sound off.
  - One colour code learned once: amber DC, blue AC, grey dead, red danger, green safe.
  - Every click also shown as a relay snap with a white flash ring.
  - The beat shown as rings travelling down the wire.
- **Meera:** one world and one camera, travelling roof → wall → meter → pole → transformer, never jumping between unrelated diagrams. No mixed illustration styles, no generic blue "tech" stock, no human drummer or dancer characters (they read as cheap AI art).

## Panel's look
- **Mix:** about 60% flat 2D with grain and warm light, 20% 3D cutaways in the same palette, and 20% real footage only where real things exist (rooftop, fan, lineman, technician with a meter, net meter), never posed as a specific incident.
- **Setting:** an Indian lane.
  - Flat roofs with an elevated GI panel frame, a black tank, tulsi.
  - A concrete pole with messy LT spaghetti.
  - A double-pole transformer with the 11 kV line above.
  - A wall net-meter box, modular switchboard, 3-pin sockets, white ceiling fans.
  - No brands and no clichés.
- **Colour:** amber DC, blue AC, grey dead, red (sparingly) danger, green safe. "What if" scenes get a dashed border and desaturation.
- **Text:** one label at a time, plus burned-in captions in a slim band.
- **Recurring idea, the beat:**
  - **The drummer** is a stylised **dhol icon on the transformer**. Each beat sends a blue ring down the street wire, one ring per wave crest.
  - **The dancer** is the **solar box with a small sun-badge face**. When it is locked to the beat, its ribbon-wave overlaps the grid wave with a green glow.
  - **For Arjun's honesty:** an oscilloscope strip shows the grid trace and the box trace.
  - **The motif's journey:** introduced at 0:56. The dhol goes silent at the cut, tests at 1:59, flies to the battery at 2:53, and fades at 3:16.

## Moments that win
1. 0:06, blazing panels above a stopping fan.
2. 0:21, "not this": the home inverter slid aside.
3. 1:25, 230 V → 11,000 V counting in the transformer.
4. 2:08–2:20, the off-beat split screen (Short-ready).
5. 2:53, the dhol flies to the battery and the fans restart.

## Mistakes to avoid
1. Wrong waveforms or flow (sine on DC, AC as a river, current after the box is off, HT→LT, real-time 50 Hz strobing).
2. Wrong Indian hardware (sloped roofs, US pole-pigs or plugs, analogue meter backwards, home inverter shown as the grid-tied box).
3. Showing an electrocution, or AI people passed off as footage. The endangered lineman stays stylised and freezes before contact.
4. A hypothetical that looks like fact (the what-if must look different).
5. Overclaiming the hybrid (show it feeding the house circuits, not a mansion), or teaching the bypass (no real menu or steps). No panel voltage number unless correct: "STILL LIVE" is safer.
