Two in the afternoon. Full sun on your roof... and the power goes.

Your panels are full of power. But your fans still stop.

Strange, right? I'll show you why, where that sunlight goes, and the one switch that fixes it.

Your inverter has one job. It turns the panels' steady current into the fifty hertz power your pole gives you.

But it can't make that beat by itself. Think of the grid as a drummer... and your inverter as a dancer that only follows the drum.

Fifty times a second, the grid swings. The box watches every swing and pushes its current out in step.

Your house takes what it needs. The extra flows out through your meter, to the street wire and the transformer on the pole.

Now the power company cuts your line.

Here's the catch. If your inverter kept pushing, that dead wire would be alive again. The pole box would step your two hundred thirty volts up to about eleven thousand — on a wire a worker is about to touch.

So the inverter is built to notice. Is the beat missing? Is the voltage drifting? It stops and opens its relay... click. The rule gives it two seconds, at most.

What if your house uses exactly what your panels make? Many of them push their own beat off a tiny bit, on purpose. The grid pulls it back. No grid... it keeps drifting, and the box trips.

So what happens to your panels? The circuit is open, so no current flows. Power is voltage times current. Zero current... means zero power.

The sunlight still lands. It just turns into heat, and the panels are fine sitting like that.

So how do you keep your fans on? A hybrid inverter with a battery.

When the grid dies, a switch inside first cuts your house off from the street. Then the battery becomes the drummer, and the panels dance again.

Here's the rule. An on-grid inverter must go silent when the grid goes.

Break it... and the man fixing your street takes the shock.

Your solar never failed. It just lost its drummer.
