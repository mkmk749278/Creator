Two in the afternoon. Full sun on your rooftop solar... and suddenly, a power cut.

The sun is still shining. But your fans stop. Your solar isn't broken. It switched off on purpose — to protect someone's life.

I'll show you why, where that sunlight goes, and what keeps your fans running.

One thing first. Not the battery inverter you use at home — I mean the box on your wall that your panels connect to. Installers call it on-grid. We'll call it the solar box.

Your panels give D.C. current. It flows in one direction only.

Your house needs A.C. current, which goes back and forth — fifty times every second. The solar box turns one into the other.

But it can't make that swing by itself. Think of the grid as a drummer... and the solar box as a dancer that only follows the drum.

On a normal day, it follows every beat. Your house takes what it needs, and the extra goes out through your meter... to the street wire and the transformer on the pole.

Now the electricity board cuts your line.

Think about it. If your solar box kept pushing, that dead wire would be alive again, right?

And the transformer would take your two hundred thirty volts... and push it up to about eleven thousand volts. On a wire the lineman thinks is dead.

So the solar box is built to notice. If the beat goes missing, or the voltage starts changing, it switches itself off... click. By the safety rules, it must do this within two seconds.

But sometimes your house uses exactly as much as your panels make. Then, when the power cut comes, the box feels no change at all. So how does it know? It keeps testing — every moment, it pushes its own beat slightly off, on purpose.

With the grid there, the grid pulls it back.

With the grid gone, nobody pulls it back... the beat drifts away, and the box switches off.

So what happens to your panels? With the box off, current has no path. So no power comes out.

The sunlight still lands and just turns into heat — your panels are built for that. But their wires still carry voltage, so only a trained person should touch them.

So how do you keep your fans running? You need a different box — a hybrid inverter. Hybrid simply means it works with the grid and with a battery.

When the grid dies, a switch inside it first cuts your house off from the street. Then the battery becomes the drummer, and the panels dance again.

So remember this. A solar box without a battery must go silent when the grid goes.

If anyone tampers with this safety feature... the lineman fixing your street takes the shock.

Your solar never failed. It just lost its drummer.
