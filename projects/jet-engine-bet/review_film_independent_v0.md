# Independent cut review: "The Blade That Should Melt", film_v0_720p.mp4 (8:30)

Method: 128 frames at 4 s intervals (accurate-seek, every one viewed), plus about 90 extra frames at 0.5 to 1 s steps around every shot change, label change and suspect moment. Voice timings come from `align.json` (63 lines). I could not hear the audio, so "voice says" is the transcript at the aligned time. Timecodes are m:ss of the video file.

## 1. Scores

- **Story clarity: 6.5/10.** The spine (empty exhaust, blade that should melt, three tricks, spin, the Kaveri history, the Safran choice) is easy to follow in the first 5 minutes, but the four-stroke explainer, the 1998 sanctions beat and the whole Kaveri chapter are not locked to the voice, and several numbers on screen disagree with it.
- **Visual realism: 5/10.** The blade, cooling x-ray and coating cross-section are convincing, but the hangar jets are generic straight-wing trainers rather than Tejas, the bus is a toy, the engine fly-through is a looped stock-style clip, and there are literal "PHOTO SLOT" and black-hole placeholders at 7:03 to 7:09.
- **Cinematic quality: 5.5/10.** The opening dolly and the globe and wireframe graphics have a premium look, but the film is two-thirds slow dissolves between 20 to 60 s holds of the same asset, with hard cuts, near-black glitch frames, inconsistent letterboxing and a dead-air ending.
