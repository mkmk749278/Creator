# Where Be Practical with Kishore has an edge in India (research, 2026-10-21)

**Short answer.** You are right: our long documentaries work, and the 2–3 minute films don't yet. But length alone
doesn't explain it. What explains it is **topic, packaging and the first 45 seconds**. The chip film won because it
was a **fresh "India's missing piece" story with money at stake**, shown on a thumbnail with one clear object. The jet
engine film lost because **dozens of channels have already answered "why can't India build a jet engine"**, and its
thumbnail text was hidden behind the timestamp. The open space is a **series on India's hidden dependencies and the
race to fix them**: rare earth magnets, medicine ingredients (APIs), AI chips and models, fertiliser, satellite
navigation. Indians type these questions into YouTube every day, and nobody has made a proper English film on them.

**How this was measured.** 95 YouTube searches (top 20 results each, views on 2026-10-21), about 440 YouTube
autocomplete phrases for India (what people actually type, `gl=IN`), our own channel list (owner's Studio screenshot,
2026-10-21), and web sources. Raw data: `docs/research/research_notes/India content edge 2026-10-21/`. **Limits:**
search results show total views, not views a month or upload dates, and YouTube blocked per-video details (likes,
dates) from the cloud. Autocomplete shows *that* people search a phrase, not *how many*. Every number in the topic
pitches below is a planning note and must go through `/fact-check` before it is used.

1. **Only "India's capability" films get pushed.** Chip + jet engine = about 93% of all our views (7.7K of about 8.3K). Studio (17 Oct)
   showed 96–99% of their views come from the home feed and suggested videos, not search. YouTube recommends these.
2. **Money and rights films get nothing.** Four credit card and insurance films have about 515 views between them.
   Big Hindi finance channels own those searches, and a home-feed viewer who liked the chip film doesn't click a
   credit card thumbnail. Each of those uploads also tells YouTube our channel is "about" many things.
3. **Kavach had almost no demand.** YouTube autocomplete for "kavach" returns only a movie and a TV serial: nobody is
   typing it. The best Kavach video anywhere has 90K views (a 1-minute current-affairs clip). Low competition there
   meant low interest, not an opening.
4. **The problem inside our good films is the first 45 seconds** (Studio, PLAYBOOK §4a-7): two thirds of the viewers
   we lose are gone by 0:45, and the rest mostly watch to the end. A shorter film doesn't fix a weak first minute. A
   strong first minute does.

**Lesson:** choose questions that **many people ask and few have answered well**, frame them as a **live bet India
can win**, and put **one object, one number and 2–4 words** on the thumbnail, clear of the bottom-right corner.

## 3. What Indians are searching for

YouTube autocomplete (India, English) for "why india can't make …": **jet engine, semiconductors, fighter jets,
aeroplane, games, anime**, plus "why can't india build its own **deepseek**". Other India phrases: "why india is behind
in technology", "why india is not developed like china", "why india imports **fertilizer / urea / edible oil / coal**",
"india **rare earth magnets** / minerals / discovery / reserves", "**amca engine**", "india **6th gen** fighter", "how
india became nuclear power", "how is indian currency made", "how are indian coins / passports made", "why indian
trains are slow / late / dented", "why india doesn't have oil / skyscrapers / bombers", "why indian passport is weak",
"why indian rupee is falling".

People ask these as **"why can't India…"**, but the films that win answer with **"here is what's blocking it and who
is fixing it"**. That's the pride the viewer wants: knowing the real reason, then seeing India working on it.

### Who we're up against, and our real edge
- **Hindi giants** (Dhruv Rathee, Vigyan Recharge, Khan GS, Mohak Mangal, Abhi and Niyu, Think School) own the big
  topics. Most are Hindi or Hinglish, 15–30 minutes, presenter-led.
- **English India-tech** coverage is mostly **news segments** (Firstpost Vantage, WION, Mint, DD India: 2–6 minutes,
  no mechanism) or **foreign channels** (The B1M, Bloomberg, Wendover) that tell India's story from outside.
- **3D "how it works" channels** (AiTelly, LifeAda, Sabin Civil Engineering) get millions with 6–10 minute
  mechanism animations, but no story, stakes or sources.

**Our edge is the combination nobody has:** a proper documentary in **simple English an Indian from any state can
follow** (South India and English-medium viewers who don't watch Hindi), **India's story told from inside India**,
with a **real mechanism animated** (our blueprint/Blender work), **primary documents on screen** and an **honest but
proud** ending. Hindi channels can't reach the non-Hindi audience, news channels don't explain the mechanism, 3D
channels don't tell a story.

## 5. Recommended slate (in order)

One lane, **"India's Missing Pieces"**: one thing India can't make yet, why (the physics, cost or supply chain),
and who in India is fixing it. The chip film becomes episode 1 of the playlist after the fact, and each film
ends by teasing the next.

1. **Rare earth magnets.** *"India Has the Rare Earths. China Has the Magnets. Why?"* Hook: an Indian EV or scooter
   line waiting for a magnet the size of a coin. Mechanism: why separating 17 near-identical elements is so hard
   (many solvent-extraction stages: count to verify), then magnet making. Stakes: China's export curbs and the one-year pause
   from late 2025 (**check whether it ends around now: news window**). Pride path: IREL, Indian magnet plants and the
   government scheme (amount to verify).
2. **Pharma APIs.** *"The Pharmacy of the World Can't Make Its Own Medicine Ingredients"*: paracetamol from
   chemical to tablet, where China comes in, and the bulk-drug parks (figures to verify).
3. **Sovereign AI.** *"Can India Build Its Own DeepSeek?"*: what training a model really needs (GPUs, data, tokens
   in Indian languages), what Sarvam, BharatGen and the IndiaAI GPU pool have actually done.
4. **India's own chip design** (sequel to film 1): Shakti and the Indian-designed chips; "designed here, made where?"
5. **Fertiliser.** *"Why India Imports the Fertiliser That Feeds It"*: natural gas → ammonia → urea, and nano urea
   claims checked honestly (contested: attribute).
6. **NavIC.** *"India Built Its Own GPS. Why Isn't Your Phone Using It?"*: atomic clocks, failures, the fix.
7. **Gaganyaan G1**, timed to the launch window (confirm date): how the capsule survives re-entry.
8. **AMCA's engine** (sequel to the jet film, only if 1–4 hold). Reuse the jet film's numbers (series continuity).

**Pause:** more credit card, insurance and railway-safety long films on this channel. Money and rights topics
become Shorts or ride inside a story (already a rule, §4a-7). Never two films on the same topic within a fortnight.

## 6. Length and format

- **What wins in this niche is 9–16 minutes:** Think School 13–21 min, Mohak Mangal 16–28, Aevy TV 19–24, Vigyan
  Recharge 16–20, The B1M 8–13, Sabin Civil 8–11, AiTelly 6–10. In 95 searches, the 2–3 minute films that rank for big India questions
  are news segments or podcast clips riding a news spike, not standalone explainers.
- YouTube's long-form average view is about 2.5 minutes across the platform (Metricool 2026), so a 3-minute film can't
  earn more than about 3 minutes of watch time per view. A 10-minute film that holds 35% earns 3.5 minutes.
  Monetisation needs **4,000 watch hours** (at 2:20 a view, about 1 lakh views), and short films make that
  much slower.
- **Recommendation:** go back to **9–14 minute documentaries** (topic decides, as before), and **keep the best parts
  of the 180-second format inside them**: zero-buffer hook, one mechanism per chapter, the HUD, real cross-sections,
  "Rule → Ruin → Reality", the bridge to the next film. Cut each film's mechanism chapter into **2–3 vertical Shorts
  (30–50 s)** that link to the full film.
- The 50-film 180-second roadmap (`config/roadmap-180s.yaml`) can become the **Shorts and chapter bank**: many of
  its mechanisms (how UPI settles, how a train brakes) make good 45-second Shorts.
- **This changes a CLAUDE.md rule** (180-second films as the main format, 2026-10-20). I have not edited the rule;
  it needs your yes.

## 7. Narrating for Indians: make them proud, honestly

1. **Paradox → honest bottleneck → Indian hands fixing it → what it means for you.** Pride comes from understanding
   and from real Indian engineers, places and plants (Sanand, Dholera, IREL's Chavara and Aluva units, ISRO's
   Mahendragiri test stands), never from hype words ("SHOCKED the world", "China is TERRIFIED"). Those titles get
   clicks on other channels, but they cost trust, and our edge is trust.
2. **Bet framing beats verdict framing.** "India's ₹X crore bet to make magnets" or "Can India build its own
   DeepSeek?" keeps the outcome open, so viewers stay to see if India wins. "Why India failed" ends the story
   before it starts.
3. **A named Indian person** within the first 20 s when possible (a scientist, plant head, startup founder), with face and
   name (already a rule). Studio data and every big Indian channel show people follow people.
4. **Indian scales:** rupees and crores first, then one comparison every Indian knows ("the whole Chandrayaan-3
   mission cost about ₹X crore; one chip fab costs N Chandrayaans": numbers to verify). Kirana, monsoon, Diwali
   and railway analogies, never US-centric ones.
5. **Pan-India English, no Hindi tags** (already a rule). This is our moat: a viewer in Chennai, Kochi, Guwahati or
   Hyderabad who skips Hindi videos can watch ours.
6. **Honest about the gap, never cynical about India** (already in the owner's guide). Name the bottleneck clearly,
   then show the fix and a date to watch for.
7. **Voice test (recommendation).** Viewers may notice the synthetic voice (open question in §4a-7). YouTube allows
   AI narration with original research, but a real Indian voice builds trust faster. Worth testing one film with a
   human voice (yours recorded on the phone, or a paid voice artist: cost to quote before trying) and comparing
   0:30 retention.

## 8. Search and packaging

1. **Title = the words people type, front-loaded.** Put the phrase from autocomplete in the first 40 characters:
   "India Has Rare Earths. Why Can't It Make Magnets?", "Can India Build Its Own DeepSeek?". Phones show about 50–60
   characters; the "| Be Practical With Kishore" suffix (your 2026-10-17 decision) uses 27 of them. The chip title
   that broke out had none. Worth testing one film without it, with the channel name in the description instead.
2. **Thumbnail:** one object, one number, 2–4 words, text in the top or left two thirds (never under the timestamp
   badge, bottom-right). Check it at 168×94 px before upload. Use YouTube's **Test & Compare** (3 thumbnails) on every
   film.
3. **Description line 1** repeats the search phrase in a plain sentence. Chapters named with search words ("How rare
   earths are separated", "Why China controls 90%": numbers to verify).
4. **Publish into news windows:** rare earth curbs, Gaganyaan G1, budget and defence deals. A news spike plus an
   evergreen explainer gets both search and home-feed traffic.
5. **One lane on the main channel.** Mixed topics confuse who YouTube shows us to. Put the series in one playlist
   ("India's Missing Pieces") and link each film's end screen to the next.
6. **Shorts:** 2–3 per film from day one (0 Shorts views so far). Shorts don't count toward the 4,000 hours, but they
   bring subscribers.
7. **Other languages later:** YouTube's multi-language audio and auto-dubbing (Hindi included) appear to be open to
   Partner Program creators (secondary sources, 2025–26). Once eligible, one film with Hindi and Telugu audio tracks
   gets all its views on one video. Check in Studio → Languages whether "Add audio track" is already available.
   We already make Telugu dubs.
