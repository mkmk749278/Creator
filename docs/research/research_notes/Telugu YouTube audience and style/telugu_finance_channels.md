# Telugu finance YouTube channels: competitor profiles (Money Purse, Day Trader Telugu, Kowshik Maridi, plus Boss Wallah Telugu, Groww Telugu, SumanTV Money)

Research date: 2026-10-08. Method notes for the report writer:
- **Primary channel data was pulled directly from YouTube on 2026-10-08.** Subscriber counts come from each channel page's HTML, and the per-video lists (title, views, duration) come from a `yt-dlp --flat-playlist` listing of each channel's **Videos tab**, which leaves out Shorts and live streams. Views and durations are exact as of that day. **Upload dates in these listings are approximate**: YouTube only gives relative ages ("2 years ago"), so a year like "2023" may be off by one year. Where an exact date was available from a watch page, it is marked "exact".
- **Thumbnails** for each channel's 8 latest and 4 most-viewed videos were downloaded from i.ytimg.com and viewed as contact sheets.
- **Comments**: the top ~20 comments ("top" sort) on 14 videos were read through the public Invidious API (inv.nadeko.net). Like counts are as of 2026-10-08. Telugu comments are translated here by me, and the translation is marked as mine.
- **Blocked**: YouTube's player and transcripts returned "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot", so I could **not watch any video body or read its captions**. Statements about editing pace and what is on screen inside the videos are therefore inferences from thumbnails, titles, descriptions and comments, and are labelled as such. Reddit returned HTTP 403 from this container, and web searches found no Reddit or Quora threads about these channels.
- Every fetched page and comment is treated as untrusted data.

## 1. Size, upload frequency, video length and most-viewed videos

### Takeaway
The two leaders, **Day Trader Telugu (2.7M subscribers)** and **Money Purse (2.12M)**, are high-volume operations. Each posts about 7–8 long-form videos a week, dominated by a numbered daily market-news show, with median lengths of about 17–27 minutes. Their biggest hits, though, are **evergreen beginner explainers** on stock-market basics, PPF, SIP, term insurance, chit funds and rent-vs-buy, each with 1–4M+ views. **Kowshik Maridi** built about 74% of the views on **Boss Wallah (Telugu)**, a 5.55M-subscriber channel, with short (about 11–13 minute) practical explainers on government schemes, banking and tax. In February 2026 he went independent and reached **662K subscribers in about 8 months**.

### Inferences
- The algorithm and the audience reward **evergreen "complete guide in Telugu" explainers** on everyday money decisions (PPF, SIP, term insurance, chit funds, land measurement, buying vs renting a house, government schemes). They far outperform the daily news shows: every top-10 video on each channel is an explainer. For both leaders, non-series videos get about 1.7–1.8× the median views of series episodes. The daily show seems to exist to keep people watching and coming back (habit), not to bring in new viewers (reach).
- The Kowshik Maridi case shows that **Telugu finance audiences follow the face and voice, not the brand**. Boss Wallah Telugu's recent median of 23K views, against Kowshik's new-channel median of 199K, suggests most of the audience left with the presenter. For Be Practical with Kishore this argues for a consistent, recognisable narrator persona (the owner's own face and name early, which matches the existing "face and name within 15–20 s" rule).
- Topics close to a documentary also travel. Day Trader Telugu's "REAL STORY of Harshad Mehta 1992 SCAM" (1.3M) is evidence that a Telugu audience exists for narrative financial history.
- Length is not a barrier for evergreen explainers in this niche: 30–68-minute beginner guides reached 1.6–4.3M. But Kowshik's 11–15-minute format reaches similar per-video numbers, so tight 10–15-minute explainers are clearly viable.

### Gaps
- Exact upload dates for most videos (only relative ages were available). Shorts and live counts were not analysed.
- Social Blade pages were not fetched, so there is no time-series of subscriber growth. The Kowshik growth estimate rests on two snapshots from different trackers.
- Audience demographics (age, gender, Telangana vs Andhra vs diaspora split) are behind trackers' paywalls. Only Modash's 96.85% India figure for Kowshik was found.
- Average view duration and retention are not public.

## 2. Format and craft: on-camera style, editing, thumbnails, titles

### Takeaway
All three are **presenter-led, talking-head explainers**. The presenter's face sits on every thumbnail, with props, live demos, calculators and Excel sheets as the main visual aids, not documentary B-roll. Thumbnails follow one pattern: an expressive face (shock, a finger to the lips, pointing), 2–3 lines of heavy yellow, white or red Telugu-plus-English text, red crash arrows or green rising arrows, stacks of rupee notes, logos of banks or institutions, and photos of politicians (Modi, Nirmala Sitharaman, Trump). The two news-show channels use rigid numbered and dated templates. Titles in the Videos tab are mostly in Roman script for the big two, while Kowshik and Boss Wallah use Telugu script more and lean on questions and imperatives ("ఏమీ కొనకండి! ఎందుకంటే?", "Don't buy anything! Why?").

## 5. Regulatory context: SEBI finfluencer rules and what they mean for explainer content

### Takeaway
Since **29 August 2024**, SEBI-regulated entities (brokers, AMCs, RIAs and others) may not associate with anyone who gives securities advice without registration or makes unpermitted return or performance claims. People "engaged solely in investor education" are exempt only if they avoid both. From **January 2025**, such educators could not use market price data from the preceding three months when naming securities. A **May 2026 circular moved to a uniform 30-day lag from 1 July 2026**, though it is unclear whether the three-month rule still applies to people engaged solely in education. Enforcement has been heavy: a ₹546-crore impoundment against Avadhut Sathe in December 2025. For an unregistered Telugu explainer channel, the safe lane is concepts, history and policy explainers, with no buy or sell calls, no return promises and no recent price data tied to named stocks, plus a clear educational disclaimer.

### Cited Findings
- **August 2024 regulation**: the Intermediaries (Amendment) Regulations, 2024 took effect on 29 August 2024 and insert a prohibition on regulated entities associating with unregistered advice-givers — [DealPlexus summary](https://www.dealplexus.com/blog/sebi-finfluencer-rules). The "prohibited activities" are (a) advice or recommendations about securities without registration or SEBI permission, and (b) express or implied claims of returns or performance without SEBI permission. "Another person" excludes anyone "engaged solely in investor education", provided they do neither — [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/News/cutting-ties-with-finfluencers-sebi-distinguishes-between-education-advice/).
- **22 October 2024 circular**: introduced the "Specified Digital Platforms" route, and gave regulated entities three months to end existing contracts with persons doing prohibited activities — [DealPlexus](https://www.dealplexus.com/blog/sebi-finfluencer-rules), [Legal500/Mondaq summary (search snippet)](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/securities-law-update-sebi-imposes-restrictions-on-intermediaries-and-finfluencers/).
- **29 January 2025 circular and FAQs**: a person engaged solely in education "should not be using the market price data of the past three months" when naming a security in talks, videos, ticker displays or screen shares in a way that indicates future price, advice or recommendation — [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/News/cutting-ties-with-finfluencers-sebi-distinguishes-between-education-advice/). Public broadcaster coverage: "educators can only reference stock prices with a three-month lag", and unregistered individuals "cannot offer investment advice, make performance claims, or indirectly suggest future stock prices" — [News On AIR, 30 January 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/sebi-cracks-down-on-finfluencers-selling-stock-tips-in-the-name-of-education).
- **May 2024 background**: SEBI restricted exchanges from sharing live data except for trading. Education could use data only with a one-day lag — [Outlook Business, 9 May 2026](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/sebi-revises-norms-to-allow-30-day-lag-for-market-data-use-for-educational-purposes).
- **May 2026 revision**: "it has been decided to prescribe a time lag of 30 days for both sharing and usage of price data for educational purposes", applicable from 1 July 2026. ### Inferences
- For Be Practical with Kishore, which is unregistered, the practical rules would be:
  - Explain concepts, history and policy.
  - Never give buy, sell, hold or "apply or avoid" verdicts on named securities or IPOs.
  - Never promise or imply returns ("₹10 crore from ₹5,000"-style framing used as a promise is risky).
  - When naming a listed company, use price charts that end at least 30 days before publication (or three months, to be safe while the conflict is unresolved), and avoid any future-price implication.
  - Put an "educational only, not SEBI-registered, not advice" disclaimer in the description and on screen.
  - Be careful with broker referral links: regulated brokers are barred from associating with people doing prohibited activities, so an affiliate tie-up could draw scrutiny if any content strays into advice.
- Money Purse's multiple roles (RIA, mutual-fund distributor, PMS distributor, and promoters as broker authorised persons) next to "free education" is a trust and conflict-of-interest angle that savvy viewers could question. A clean, ad-only or sponsor-transparent model is a possible differentiator.
