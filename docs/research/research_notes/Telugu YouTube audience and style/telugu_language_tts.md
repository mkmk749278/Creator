# Telugu language for YouTube narration and ElevenLabs TTS

Scope: register, dialect, code-mixing, idioms, sensitive words and TTS-friendly spelling for a Telugu documentary/explainer channel (Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, diaspora). Researched 2026-10-08. Fetched pages were treated as untrusted data. Where a point comes from general linguistic knowledge rather than a fetched source, it is placed under **Inferences** and marked for the owner (a native speaker) to confirm.

## 1. Vyavaharika vs grandhika: history, modern media use, what sounds bookish

### Takeaway
Modern written and broadcast Telugu is the descendant of Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy's *vyavaharika* (spoken/standard modern) movement; *grandhika* (Sanskrit-heavy literary Telugu) now survives mainly in classical poetry and ceremonial contexts. Narration should be educated spoken Telugu: the register of TV news and Eenadu-style journalism, not textbook or kavya style.

### Cited Findings
- Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy (1863–1940), born 29 August 1863 in Parvatalapeta, Srikakulam; taught at Gajapati Maharaja School, Parlakimidi; nicknamed "Pidugu" (thunderbolt). — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/lifestyle/culture-and-society/telugu-language-day-commemorates-gidugu-revolutionary-behind-simple-telugu-usage-1983163)
- Grandhika bhasha was "heavily influenced by Sanskrit"; Gidugu sought to make written Telugu accessible through vyavaharika bhasha (spoken Telugu), believing school Telugu should reflect what native speakers actually use; his efforts "helped establish Telugu as a medium of teaching, writing and examination". — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/lifestyle/culture-and-society/telugu-language-day-commemorates-gidugu-revolutionary-behind-simple-telugu-usage-1983163)
- He argued the grandhika taught in schools and colleges did not build real-life comprehension/communication skills; the dispute with orthodox Sanskrit-educated pundits "sometimes turned vitriolic". (Wikipedia flags parts of this section as under-cited.) — [Wikipedia: Gidugu Venkata Ramamurthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gidugu_Venkata_Ramamurthy)
- His birthday, 29 August, is Telugu Language Day (తెలుగు భాషా దినోత్సవం) in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Yanam. AP Official Language Act passed 1966; Telugu got classical-language status in 2008. — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/lifestyle/culture-and-society/telugu-language-day-commemorates-gidugu-revolutionary-behind-simple-telugu-usage-1983163)
- Telangana separately marks 9 September as Telangana Language Day (declared 2014, the birth anniversary of poet Kaloji Narayana Rao). — [Wikipedia: Telangana dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_dialect); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/opinion/ego-over-enrichment-andhras-lingering-claim-on-pure-telugu-exposed-by-idupu-kayitham/)
- "Since the 1930s, what was considered an 'elite' literary form of the Telugu language has now spread to the common people"; this form is taught in schools and colleges as a standard. — [Wikipedia: Telugu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language)
- Eenadu (launched 10 August 1974, Visakhapatnam, by Ramoji Rao) grew partly through "its use of regional dialects, colloquialisms, idioms and sarcasm" in reporting. — [Wikipedia: Eenadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eenadu)
- Eenadu also ran a rigorous Eenadu Journalism School focused on language skills, with recruits reading classics and doing translation exercises: colloquial but disciplined. — [The News Minute, Ramoji Rao legacy](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/andhra-pradesh/ramoji-rao-the-media-man-a-formidable-and-mixed-legacy)

### Inferences
- Target register = "educated spoken standard": the Telugu of a TV-news explainer or a well-edited newspaper feature read aloud, not the inflected written forms of textbooks. (Inference from the above plus general knowledge; owner to confirm with reference channels.)
- Features that typically sound bookish/grandhika when read aloud (general linguistic knowledge, **not verified from a fetched source**; owner to confirm):
  - Classical verb endings and case markers: `వచ్చెను` (came, literary) vs spoken `వచ్చాడు/వచ్చింది`; `చేయుచున్నాడు` (is doing, literary) vs `చేస్తున్నాడు`; `అతనియొక్క` vs `అతని`.
  - Final `-ము/-వు` noun endings: `రాజ్యము`, `ధనము` vs spoken `రాజ్యం`, `ధనం`; `పుస్తకము` vs `పుస్తకం`.
  - Dense Sanskrit tatsama compounds where a common word exists: `ఆరోగ్య పరిరక్షణ విధానము` where `ఆరోగ్యం కాపాడుకునే పద్ధతి` would do; `ప్రారంభించబడింది` (passive calque) vs `మొదలైంది`.
  - Passive `-బడు` constructions (a translation-from-English pattern) generally sound stiff; prefer active voice.
- Equally, avoid the opposite extreme (heavy street slang or strongly regional colloquial forms) for a neutral documentary voice: see §2.

## 2. Regional variation and a neutral standard; post-2014 sensitivities

### Takeaway
The de facto standard (and the base of media/school Telugu) is the educated Central/Coastal (Krishna–Godavari–Guntur) variety, but since the Telangana movement that standard is politically loaded: Telangana speech was long mocked in cinema as crude or villainous. A neutral documentary voice should use widely understood standard forms, avoid marking any region as "correct", never use a regional dialect for comic, crude or villain framing, and treat Telangana and Andhra vocabulary as equals.

### Cited Findings
- Four regional dialect groups (per *A Grammar of Modern Telugu*): Northern (Telangana), Southern (Rayalaseema), Central (Coastal Andhra), Eastern (North Andhra/Uttarandhra). "Standard Telugu is based on the speech of educated speakers of the Central dialect." — [Wikipedia: Telugu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language)
- Pronunciation markers: Telangana dialects merge ś → ṣ/s (e.g. `దేశం` dēśam → dēsaw̃) and use fewer aspirated consonants; the plural of `కన్ను` (eye) is *kaḷḷu* (`కళ్ళు`) in Coastal, *kaṇḍḷu/kaṇḷu* in Rayalaseema, *kanlu* in Telangana. — [Wikipedia: Telugu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language)
- Telangana vocabulary (Hindustani/Urdu-influenced) vs standard: `కిరాయి` kirāyi vs `అద్దె` adde (rent); `దవాఖానా` davākhānā vs `ఆసుపత్రి` āsupatri (hospital); `జాగా` jāgā vs `ప్రాంతం`/`స్థలం` (place/area); `జల్దీ` jaldi vs `తొందర` tondara (quick); `పైసలు` paisalu vs `డబ్బులు` ḍabbulu (money); `ఆలుగడ్డ` ālugaḍḍa vs `బంగాళదుంప` baṅgāḷadumpa (potato); `పైలం` pailaṁ vs `జాగ్రత్త` jāgratta (be careful); `దూప` dūpa vs `దాహం` dāhaṁ (thirst); `ఏడ/ఆడ/ఈడ` (where/there/here). — [Wikipedia: Telangana dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_dialect)
- Modern Standard Telugu is described as based on the speech of erstwhile Krishna, Guntur, East and West Godavari districts; Telugu films used coastal (Godavari/Krishna) dialects as de facto standard. — [Wikipedia: Telangana dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_dialect) (via search summary); [Anveshi, Elavarthi & Vemireddy](https://www.anveshi.org.in/?p=2238)
- A Telangana writer argues Telangana students were taught a "standardized" Telugu of Godavari, Krishna and Guntur that was "a foreign language to the people of Telangana". — [Anveshi, Sangisetti Srinivas](https://anveshi.org.in/?p=2236)
- From the early 1990s Telangana dialect was used in films "as a tool for ridicule", to signal crude or villainous characters or comic relief (e.g. Kota Srinivasa Rao in *Jayammu Nischayammu Raa* with a donkey bray in the background; the "ignorant housewife" in *Mondi Mogudu Penki Pellam*; the cruel grandmother in *Nuvvu Nenu*). — [Anveshi](https://www.anveshi.org.in/?p=2238)
- Henchmen, sidekicks and comic characters "invariably" spoke Telangana dialect; actor Shakuntala was typecast as a foul-mouthed lady don ("Telangana Shakuntala"). Film industry was largely run by people from coastal Andhra. — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/fidaa-ismart-shankar-rise-telangana-dialect-mainstream-cinema-107328); [Film Companion](https://filmcompanion.in/readers-articles/telugu-movies-the-joy-of-celebrating-the-telangana-bidda-dialect-in-telugu-cinema-jathi-ratnalu-pitta-kathalu)
- The statehood movement turned the dialect into an assertion of identity "in the face of standardization and false homogeneity of the universal Telugu identity promoted by all media industries". Post-2014 films (*Pelli Choopulu*, *Fidaa*, *Balagam*) mainstreamed it. — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/fidaa-ismart-shankar-rise-telangana-dialect-mainstream-cinema-107328); [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/mockery-over-idupu-kayitham-reveals-telanganas-unfinished-fight-for-dignity)
- The debate is live in 2026: the film *Idupu Kayitham* drew sneers from some Andhra quarters ("what language is this?"); Telangana Today frames this as Telangana's "unfinished fight for dignity"; The South First says Telangana speech was dismissed as "Hyderabadi slang" or "Urdu-mixed". Critics of *The Paradise* say films still link Telangana characters with alcohol, meat, foul language and rugged behaviour. — [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/mockery-over-idupu-kayitham-reveals-telanganas-unfinished-fight-for-dignity); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/opinion/ego-over-enrichment-andhras-lingering-claim-on-pure-telugu-exposed-by-idupu-kayitham/); [M9 News](https://www.m9.news/movienews/nanis-the-paradise-telangana-portrayal-problematic/)
- Counter-view: all regional varieties "fall under the rubric of Telugu". — [Sakshi Post](https://www.sakshipost.com/was-world-telugu-conference-all-about-telangana)

### Inferences
- Rule: never call one region's Telugu "pure" (`అసలైన తెలుగు`, `స్వచ్ఛమైన తెలుగు`) or another's "slang"; this is exactly the framing the sources show causing offence.
- Rule: when a word differs by region and both are widely understood, prefer the pan-regional form (`డబ్బులు`, `ఆసుపత్రి`, `జాగ్రత్త`), but use Telangana forms naturally when the story is set in Telangana, or quoting Telangana speakers; never for comic effect.
- Rule: no dialect-as-character shorthand (villain, drunk, uneducated) in narration, reconstructions or AI voice styling.
- Name places and states neutrally and currently: "Andhra Pradesh" and "Telangana" (post-2 June 2014), "erstwhile/united Andhra Pradesh" (`ఉమ్మడి ఆంధ్రప్రదేశ్`) for the pre-2014 state. Avoid "Seemandhra" unless quoting, since it was a movement-era label (general knowledge; owner to confirm).
- Pick an ElevenLabs voice whose accent reads as neutral educated Telugu; test it with listeners from both states.

### Gaps
- No sourced list of Rayalaseema- or Uttarandhra-specific vocabulary/pronunciation was found beyond the `కళ్ళు` example.
- No sourced list of derogatory region terms (e.g. terms used against "Andhra settlers" in Hyderabad or against Telangana people) was found; owner input needed.
- No survey data on which accent diaspora or cross-state audiences find most neutral.

## 3. English code-mixing (Tenglish)

### Takeaway
Telugu–English mixing ("Tenglish") is normal and expected in educated urban speech, especially among the young, while people still say they do not want it in "official" registers. For narration: keep Telugu grammar and syntax, use English for technical/modern nouns that listeners only know in English, and avoid English function words or whole English clauses.

### Cited Findings
- Tenglish "is extensively used in Hyderabad"; it "has become an essential component of an educated bilingual in informal domains. The English words are assimilated into Telugu language so conveniently that many times it becomes difficult to get a right substitute ... not only with youngsters but also with elders. But officially, people do not want to use this code mixed variety." — [Dutta & Rajeswari 2015, Texas Linguistics Forum 58](https://asol.ling.utexas.edu/salsa/proceedings/2015/Dutta.pdf)
- Young informants could not produce Telugu equivalents for words like "doubt", "bill", "college", "doctor", "income tax"; they mixed "without changing the structure of the language", e.g. `ఇది నాకు చాలా inconvenient` ("this is very inconvenient for me") and `ఈ concept-ని నాకు explain ...` (English noun + Telugu case suffix). — [Dutta & Rajeswari 2015](https://asol.ling.utexas.edu/salsa/proceedings/2015/Dutta.pdf)
- In the same Hyderabad sample (61 informants), family/community domains used mostly Telugu and Tenglish; English dominated official domains (74% of men and 80% of women used English with bosses/teachers). — [Dutta & Rajeswari 2015](https://asol.ling.utexas.edu/salsa/proceedings/2015/Dutta.pdf)
- Code-mixing has grown with social media; a 2023 IIIT Hyderabad thesis studied code-mixing in Telugu political speeches and compiled dialect rules for Standard vs Telangana Telugu. — [IIIT Hyderabad thesis](https://web2py.iiit.ac.in/research_centres/publications/view_publication/mastersthesis/1304)
- Telugu–English tweets (politics, films, sport) are noisy, with informal transliteration and misspellings. — [Srirangam et al., ACL 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-2025/)

### Inferences
- Leave in English (written in Telugu script for TTS; see §6): everyday borrowings that have no common Telugu word or whose Telugu word sounds bookish: `డాక్టర్`, `హాస్పిటల్` (or `ఆసుపత్రి`), `కాలేజ్`, `బిల్`, `ఫోన్`, `ఇంటర్నెట్`, `వైరస్`, `బ్యాంక్`, `కంపెనీ`, `ట్యాక్స్`, `టెక్నాలజీ`, brand and product names, units (`కిలోమీటర్`, `మెగాపిక్సెల్`), scientific terms the audience learnt in English-medium school (`DNA` → `డీఎన్ఏ`, `ప్రోటీన్`, `సెల్`).
- Translate into Telugu: verbs, connectors, emotions and common nouns (`explain చేయడం` → `వివరించడం`, `start అయ్యింది` → `మొదలైంది`, `but` → `కానీ`, `actually` → `నిజానికి`). Heavy "English verb + చేయడం" sounds like casual vlog talk, fine in moderation, not for a documentary voice.
- Rule of thumb for this channel: at most one English content word per clause, never an English sentence inside a Telugu sentence; on first use of a technical term, say the Telugu gloss once (`కణం, అంటే సెల్`).

- No style guide from a Telugu broadcaster on loanword spelling was found.

## 4. Idioms, sayings and discourse markers

### Takeaway
Few authoritative online collections exist; the fetched lists were thin. A short vetted list is below, with flags where a proverb uses a disability or gender stereotype. Discourse markers are given as owner-to-confirm suggestions.

### Cited Findings
- Telugu terms: `సామెతలు` (sāmetalu, proverbs) and `జాతీయాలు` (jātīyālu, idioms). — [Ling App: Telugu idioms](https://ling-app.com/blog/telugu-idioms/)
- `పైన పటారం, లోన లొటారం`: grand outside, hollow inside ("big hat, no cattle"). — [Ling App](https://ling-app.com/blog/telugu-idioms/)
- `వేలం వెర్రి`: auction frenzy, describes chaotic craze/mania (useful for bubbles, fads). — [Ling App](https://ling-app.com/blog/telugu-idioms/)
- `అంత్య నిష్ఠూరం కన్నా ఆది నిష్ఠూరం మేలు`: better to face unpleasantness at the start than at the end. — [Wikiquote: Telugu proverbs](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Telugu_proverbs)
- `కొండ నాలుకకు మందు వేస్తే ఉన్న నాలుక ఊడిందంట`: treating the uvula cost the tongue; the cure was worse than the disease (good for policy backfires). — [Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Telugu_proverbs)
- `అనువు గాని చోట అధికుల మనరాదు`: don't assert superiority where it's out of place. — [Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Telugu_proverbs)
- `నక్క ఎక్కడ, నాగలోకం ఎక్కడ` (Wikiquote lists a దేవలోకం variant): a jackal and heaven, a hopeless mismatch. — [Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Telugu_proverbs)
- Avoid (flagged): `అంధునకు అద్దము చూపినట్టు` (showing a mirror to a blind man) and `గుడ్డివాడెరుగునా కుందనపు ఛాయ` (can a blind man know gold's sheen): disability-based; a proverb using `బోగముది` (sex worker): gender-demeaning. — [Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Telugu_proverbs)

### Inferences (native-speaker knowledge, not source-verified; owner to approve before use)
- Discourse markers that make narration conversational: `అసలు విషయం ఏంటంటే` (the real point is…), `ఇక్కడే ఒక ట్విస్ట్ ఉంది` (here's the twist), `ఇంతకీ` (so anyway / after all that), `నిజానికి` (in fact), `అంటే` (that is / meaning), `అయితే` (however / but then), `ఇక్కడ గమనించాల్సిన విషయం` (the thing to notice here), `మరి` (so then), `సరే, ఇప్పుడు…` (OK, now…), `ఒక్క నిమిషం ఆగండి` (wait a moment), `ఆశ్చర్యం ఏంటంటే` (the surprising part is), `చివరికి` (finally), `మీరు ఊహించినట్టే` (just as you guessed).
- Use respectful plural address `మీరు` with the audience (not `నువ్వు`), and the respectful forms `-గారు`/`వారు` for named elders and officials; plain `అతను/ఆమె` for neutral reporting.
- Use at most one proverb per segment, and only where it fits the meaning exactly.
- Caste-coded proverbs exist (e.g. ones that stereotype particular communities); screen every proverb for caste, religion, gender, disability or region before use.

### Gaps
- No authoritative, cited proverb dictionary (e.g. a university or Sahitya Akademi collection) was reached.
- Usage frequency of discourse markers on top Telugu explainer channels was not measured.

## 5. Words and expressions to avoid; legal context

### Takeaway
Caste names used as insults, caste-derived abuse words (`చండాలం` family), disability terms used as insults, gendered sexual slurs and region-mocking labels must be banned from scripts. Indian law (SC/ST Atrocities Act s.3(1)(r)/(s)) criminalises intentional insult of SC/ST members "in public view" by caste name, and Telugu TV journalists and a Telugu film star have faced FIRs under it.

### Cited Findings
- *Chandala* is a Sanskrit term for a person who disposes of corpses and a caste historically treated as untouchable; it is widely used as a general insult for cruelty or wickedness (cf. Kannada dictionary senses "a man ... engaged in wicked, cruel deeds"). Telugu `చండాలం` ("disgusting, awful") carries the same caste origin. — [Wikipedia: Chandala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandala); [Homegrown: casteist slurs](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-voices/casteism-in-our-words-10-casteist-slurs-and-why-we-need-to-stop-throwing-them-around); [Alar Kannada dictionary](https://alar.ink/dictionary/kannada/english/%E0%B2%9A%E0%B2%82%E0%B2%A1%E0%B2%BE%E0%B2%B2)
- Occupations tied to death or dead animals came to be considered "untouchable" as Brahmanical purity ideas intensified; casual use of such words perpetuates casteism (Homegrown also covers "chapri"). — [Homegrown](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-voices/casteism-in-our-words-10-casteist-slurs-and-why-we-need-to-stop-throwing-them-around)
- Baahubali 2 (2017): the Arekatika Porata Samithi filed a complaint at Banjara Hills police (Hyderabad) and wrote to the Censor Board over Kattappa's line "Katika Cheekati" (`కటిక చీకటి`, "pitch dark"), which they said insulted the Katika community name. Police sought legal advice; no case was registered at the time. Shows even ordinary idioms that contain a caste name can draw complaints. — [The News Minute, 1 May 2017](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/telangana/caste-group-files-police-complaint-against-alleged-slur-baahubali-writes-censor-board-61292)
- Mojo TV (Hyderabad): ex-CEO Revathi Pogadadanda was detained as an accused in an SC/ST Act case arising from a January 2019 debate in which a co-anchor raised a Dalit panelist's caste on air; the panelist said naming his caste on TV was itself discriminatory. — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/media/journalist-revathi-pogadadanda-detained-hyderabad-police)
- Sakshi TV (June 2025): journalist Kommineni Srinivasa Rao was arrested by AP police after a Dalit farmers' leader complained about a panelist's remarks on Amaravati women; the FIR named the anchor, the panelist and channel management. Shows hosts/channels can be named for guests' words. — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/media/senior-journalist-in-andhra-pradesh-arrested-over-talk-show-panelists-remarks-triggers-political-uproar); [Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/2025/06/10/sakshi-tv-journalist-arrested-over-remarks-on-amaravati-made-on-his-show)
- Actor Vijay Devarakonda was booked by Telangana police under the SC/ST Act for remarks comparing tribals to terrorists at a film event; he apologised. — [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/actor-vijay-devarakonda-booked-under-sc-st-act-for-remark-on-tribals-1503448446.html)
- A Telugu TV anchor was booked over a "whores in film industry" remark; the channel expressed regret (gender-based, not caste). — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/telugu-tv-anchor-booked-whores-film-industry-comment-channel-expresses-regret-78471)
- YouTube-specific precedent (Hindi, Delhi, 2026): YouTuber Ajeet Bharti was booked under the SC/ST Act plus IT Act s.67 and BNS ss.196(1)(c), 351(3) over a YouTube episode; trial court and Delhi High Court refused anticipatory bail, finding a prima facie case under SC/ST Act ss.3(1)(r) and 3(1)(s). — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/delhi-high-court-denies-anticipatory-bail-to-ajeet-bharti-in-scst-act-case); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/news-updates/delhi-court-denies-anticipatory-bail-to-youtuber-ajeet-bharti-in-scst-act-case-548903)
- Law: *Hitesh Verma v. State of Uttarakhand* (2020): s.3(1)(r) requires the insult to be linked to the victim's SC/ST status or an intent to humiliate, and to occur "in any place within public view". *Karuppudayar* (2025) quashed a case where abuse was not in public view. A Calcutta HC single judge held "public view" extends to social media. — [SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/all-insults-not-necessarily-offence-under-scst-act-sc/); [Supreme Court judgment, 31 Jan 2025](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2024/22333/22333_2024_2_1501_58905_Judgement_31-Jan-2025.pdf); [Vajiram & Ravi on Calcutta HC](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/?p=126982)
- Telangana HRC (Sept 2026) sought police reports on complaints of caste-based abuse by a BRS MLC against the Dalit Assembly Speaker; caste abuse remains an active political/legal issue in the state. — [UNI India](https://www.uniindia.com/south-india/law-telangana-tghrc/176701)

### Inferences
- Script ban list (expand with owner):
  - Caste-origin insults: `చండాలం/చండాలపు/చండాలుడు` (use `దారుణం`, `ఘోరం`, `అసహ్యం`, `భయంకరం` instead); any caste name used as an adjective or insult; terms of untouchability; "chapri"-style class/caste slang.
  - Disability insults: `గుడ్డివాడు`, `చెవిటివాడు`, `కుంటివాడు`, `పిచ్చోడు` as insults (use `దృష్టి లోపం ఉన్నవారు`, `వినికిడి లోపం ఉన్నవారు`, `దివ్యాంగులు`, `మానసిక అనారోగ్యం`).
  - Gendered/sexual slurs and terms like `బోగముది`, `లంజ` (never), and "women are…" generalisations.
  - Religious mockery or slurs against any community; describe beliefs neutrally ("…అని నమ్ముతారు").
  - Region-mocking labels: calling Telangana speech "slang"/"Urdu-mixed", or Andhra people derisive names; no regional accent for jokes.
- Name a person's caste only if it is essential to the story and sourced, attributed and respectfully worded (Mojo TV case: naming caste on air was itself the complaint). Prefer official terms: `షెడ్యూల్డ్ కులాలు (ఎస్సీ)`, `షెడ్యూల్డ్ తెగలు (ఎస్టీ)`, `దళితులు`, `ఆదివాసీలు/గిరిజనులు` as communities themselves use them.
- Even ordinary idioms that contain a community name (`కటిక చీకటి`) can draw complaints; prefer `కారు చీకటి`/`చిమ్మ చీకటి` (pitch dark).
- Because hosts and channels were named in FIRs for guests' words, the channel's own quotes from interviews/clips also need screening.

## 6. TTS (ElevenLabs and others) for Telugu

### Takeaway
Use a model that actually supports Telugu (Eleven v3/v4 families; *not* Multilingual v2 or Flash v2.5), always pass `language_code: "te"`, write everything in Telugu script, spell out numbers/dates/units/acronyms in Telugu words, control pacing with punctuation (v3/v4 ignore SSML `<break>`), and keep sentences short. Telugu does not delete the inherent vowel, so spelling should match how the word is spoken.

### Cited Findings
- Telugu (`tel`) is supported by Eleven v4, v4 Turbo, v3 and v3 Conversational. It is **not** listed for Multilingual v2 (29 languages incl. Hindi and Tamil), Flash v2.5 (v2 languages plus Hungarian, Norwegian, Vietnamese) or the deprecated Turbo v2.5. — [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)
- Normalisation is on by default for TTS models, but smaller models generalise worse (Flash v2.5 reads "$1,000,000" as "one thousand thousand dollars"); recommended mitigations: write numbers, dates, symbols and abbreviations out in words; add normalisation instructions to the LLM that writes the text; or preprocess with regex. Problem items: phone numbers, currency, dates, times, addresses, URLs, unit abbreviations. — [ElevenLabs normalization](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/normalization)
- "11" can be read as "Eleven", "Once" or "Elf" depending on language; ElevenLabs recommends writing numbers fully in words. — [ElevenLabs help: numbers, dates, symbols, acronyms](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/core-capabilities/text-to-speech/why-are-numbers-dates-symbols-and-acronyms-not-properly-pronounced-or-spoken-in-the-correct-language)
- `language_code` (ISO 639-1, Telugu = `te`) is optional but "Specifying the language ensures the normalizer applies the correct rules for that language", useful for short or number-only text. — [ElevenLabs help: select language and accent](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/core-capabilities/text-to-speech/how-do-i-select-the-language-and-accent)
- Eleven v4 and v3 "do not support SSML break tags"; use ellipses (pauses and weight), punctuation and text structure for pacing; capitals add emphasis; audio tags like `[whispers]` direct delivery but vary by voice. v4 accepts IPA wrapped in forward slashes for names/terms. Professional Voice Clones are "not fully optimized for v3"; v4 recommended for them. — [ElevenLabs v3/v4 prompting](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v3)
- Phoneme tags work only with `eleven_flash_v2`; alias tags and pronunciation dictionaries (TXT/.PLS, case-sensitive, first match wins) are the fallback; respelling phonetically also works. — [ElevenLabs normalization](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/normalization)
- Telugu keeps the inherent vowel (no Hindi-style schwa deletion), and contrastive gemination (e.g. double consonants) matters for intelligibility (vendor page, not peer-reviewed). — [tts.ai Telugu](https://tts.ai/text-to-speech/telugu/)
- Research: transliterating English words into the native script cut code-mix word-error from 0.80–0.85 to 0.14–0.27 for Hindi/Telugu/Tamil in an adapted multilingual TTS (Praxy Voice). — [arXiv 2604.25441 via Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.25441)
- Older Microsoft Research work on code-mixed TTS: identify each word's language, normalise spellings, and map to the voice's language phonetics. — [Sitaram et al., Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=424584)
- IIT Madras has led the Indic TTS consortium since 2009 (TTS work since 2004). — [IIT Madras IndicTTS](https://www.iitm.ac.in/donlab/indictts/publications)
- An unaudited 2026 vendor benchmark put ElevenLabs behind a leading Indic provider on code-switched Telugu and name pronunciation. — [caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/indic-tts-benchmark-bulbul-elevenlabs-sarvam-google-ai4bharat-2026) (methodology unpublished)

### Inferences (TTS script rules to test)
1. Model/config: `eleven_v3` or `eleven_v4`, `language_code: "te"`; never Multilingual v2/Flash for Telugu. Verify against the live models endpoint before each project, since lists change.
2. Script: all narration in Telugu script, including English loanwords (`టెక్నాలజీ`, not "technology"), consistent with the transliteration result above. Keep Latin script only in on-screen text, not the TTS string.
3. Numbers: spell out in Telugu words as spoken: `2014` → `రెండు వేల పద్నాలుగు`; `₹1,500 కోట్లు` → `పదిహేను వందల కోట్ల రూపాయలు`; `45%` → `నలభై ఐదు శాతం`; dates `2 జూన్ 2014` → `రెండువేల పద్నాలుగు జూన్ రెండో తేదీ`. Use Indian units (`లక్ష`, `కోటి`), not "million" (general knowledge; listen to verify).
4. Acronyms: write as spoken letter-names in Telugu script (`ఐఎస్ఆర్ఓ` or `ఇస్రో` as people say it; `డీఎన్ఏ`; `జీడీపీ`), or expand once in full.
5. Punctuation: one idea per sentence, ~8–15 words; use `,` for short breaths, `.` for stops, `...` for dramatic pause; avoid `;`, brackets, slashes, `&`, `#`, emoji in TTS text.
6. Spelling: use standard spoken spellings (`మొదలైంది`, `చేస్తున్నారు`) so the TTS doesn't produce grandhika pronunciations; mark gemination explicitly (`అమ్మ`, `పెళ్ళి`); for names the model mispronounces, try respelling or an alias pronunciation dictionary entry; on v4 try IPA in slashes.
7. QA: generate several takes; have a native listener check numbers, names, aspirates and gemination; keep a project pronunciation dictionary.
